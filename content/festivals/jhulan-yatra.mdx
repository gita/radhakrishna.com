For one month of the year the monsoon turns Braj green, and the deities come down off their altars to be swung. **Jhulan Yatra** is the swing festival of Shravana, when Radha and Krishna are seated on a jhula hung with flowers and gently pushed by everyone who comes. It is among the sweetest festivals in the Radha Krishna year, and among the least demanding: there is no fast to keep and nothing to get right. There is a rope, and you pull it.

## When Jhulan Yatra falls in 2026

Three different lengths are kept, and all three are genuine. It is worth knowing which one a temple follows before you plan around it.

| Observance | 2026 dates | Kept by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Five days**, Pavitropana Ekadashi to Purnima | 23 to 28 August | ISKCON and most temples |
| **Thirteen days**, Hariyali Teej to Purnima | 15 to 28 August | Vrindavan town, the Braj custom |
| **Seven days**, Dashami to Pratipada | Shravana | Puri Jagannath |

All of them close on **Jhulan Purnima, Friday 28 August 2026**, the full moon of Shravana. In 2026 the general and Vaishnava reckonings agree on that day, so there is no split to navigate this year.

One small thing that confuses people: the five-day form begins on Ekadashi, 23 August, but the Ekadashi fast itself is kept the next day, on dwadashi. The festival and the fast are not on the same day, and neither source is wrong.

The same Purnima is also **Balarama Jayanti**, the appearance day of Lord Balarama, so the last night of Jhulan carries a second joy with it.

## Why a swing, and why the rains

The reason is the season. After the long heat, the forests of Vrindavan bloom, the Yamuna runs full, the air turns soft and heavy with the smell of wet earth, and the cuckoos begin. In weather like that a swing moves the air over you, and the whole festival rests on that plain and tender idea: the swing is offered because it is a comfort.

That is why Jhulan is understood as **seva** rather than as a performance. The deities do not stay behind the altar to be looked at. They are brought down, set on the swing, and pushed by hand, by anyone who comes.

The swing pastime is described in the classical Gaudiya literature. **Govinda Lilamrita** of Krishnadasa Kaviraja Goswami sets a jewelled swing platform in Lalita's kunja near Radha Kund, where the branches of two bakula trees meet overhead like a canopy and a swing hangs from them, bound at four corners with silken cords. The festival is named outright in the **Nectar of Devotion**, where Srila Prabhupada lists among the ceremonies to be observed "the ceremony of Jhulana-yatra with the Lord sitting on a swing."

The calendar observance itself is carried by the temples of Braj rather than prescribed by a numbered verse, which is exactly how a living festival works.

## The jhulan as a meditation

Our own lineage teaches jhulan lila as something you can enter rather than only attend. **Swami Mukundananda** gives it as a guided meditation, a form of roop dhyan in which the mind's senses all engage at once:

> Visualize a grand Kadamba tree in the sacred forests of Vrindavan. A swing hangs from its branches, its silken ropes wound with fresh flowers, its seat cushioned with petals. Radha and Krishna sit together, resplendent and playful, their divine love filling the very air with sweetness. You come forward. With a heart full of seva, you take the rope and begin gently, lovingly, to pull.
>
> <cite>Swami Mukundananda, JKYog</cite>

He puts the point of it in a line: the jhulan lila is not only a divine pastime, it is an invitation to swing the mind in loving remembrance of Radha and Krishna. Anyone can keep the festival that way, with no swing, no murti and no temple within reach. More on the teachers this site follows is on [our lineage page](/lineage).

## What Braj does

**Banke Bihari** holds the darshan people travel for. Through the first half of Shravana the temple keeps the **Phool Bangla**, a palace of flowers rebuilt each day. Then a large **hindola** is raised on a platform outside the jagmohan, and on the evening of Hariyali Teej something happens that happens only once a year: Bihariji **leaves the sanctum**. He comes out into the courtyard with Priyaji and Swami Haridas to take the swing, dressed in green to match the season, on the **Swarna Hindola** of gold and silver. Ghevar and pheny are given as prasad.

**Shri Radha Vallabh** says of its own festival that the hindolas of Shravana are world famous in Braj, and the temple keeps the full thirteen days. The swing is redecorated every single day with fresh leaves and new curtains, and darshan runs from Sandhya Utthapan through to the end of Sandhya Aarti. Pua is offered daily, with ghevar, feni, gujhia, imarti and seasonal fruit alongside.

**Radha Raman** in Vrindavan, **Dwarkadhish** in Mathura and **Ladli Lal** in Barsana are among the other principal jhulan temples of Braj.

At **Shri Krishna Balaram Mandir**, ISKCON's temple in Vrindavan, Radha Govinda are invited into a flower-decked grove for five evenings from 23 August.

## What actually happens at a jhulan

The pattern is much the same wherever it is kept, and it is worth knowing before you go, because you are meant to take part.

The small festival deities are lifted down from the altar and placed on the swing, which is often silver or gold and hung thick with flowers. **The deities are dressed in green** through the festival, for the season. Arati is offered. Then the congregation is invited forward, and each person offers flower petals, makes their own prayer, and pushes the swing a few times while everyone else sings. Rose water is sprayed in a fine mist toward the couple.

At Mayapur the kirtan around the swing runs close to two hours, and the queue to push it does not thin.

The parallel devotees draw is with Rath Yatra: this is the other festival where the deities come down and let themselves be moved by everybody's hands.

## Jhulan in Puri and Bengal

Beyond Braj the festival is kept just as warmly. At the **Jagannath temple in Puri** it runs seven days, and Madanmohan, Jagannath's festival deity, is placed with Lakshmi and Vishwadhatri on a wooden swing on the Jhulan Mandap, where worshippers get their turn to swing the Lord. The decorations use forest creepers and jasmine. The full moon there is **Jhulan Purnima**, also called Gamha Purnima, and it is kept as the appearance day of Lord Balabhadra.

In Bengal the festival is one of the high points of the year, and at ISKCON Mayapur Radha Madhava are swung daily through it amid flowers, beads and near constant kirtan.

## Keeping Jhulan Yatra at home

This is one festival that loses nothing in a small room. Asked exactly how to do it at home, Jayapataka Swami gave the whole answer in a sentence: make a small swing, put your deities or a picture of Radha and Krishna on it, decorate with flowers, and swing it.

A shape that works:

- **Make the jhula.** A small wooden or cane swing, or a frame with two cords. Many families keep the same one year after year.
- **Decorate it fresh each day.** Flowers, leaves, a little green cloth. Changing it daily is part of the seva, and it is what the temples do.
- **Offer arati**, then take the rope yourself and swing gently while you sing. A simple kirtan is enough.
- **Offer a seasonal sweet.** Pua, ghevar or feni if you can make or find them.
- **If you have no murti**, keep the jhulan in the mind, as Swami Mukundananda teaches. It asks nothing but attention.

## Where this sits in the Radha Krishna year

Jhulan Yatra opens the great Braj season. [Janmashtami](/festivals/janmashtami) follows a week after Jhulan Purnima, [Radhashtami](/festivals/radhashtami) a fortnight after that, and [Sharad Purnima](/festivals/sharad-purnima) closes the monsoon in October with the night of the maha raas.

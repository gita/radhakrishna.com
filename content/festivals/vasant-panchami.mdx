Somewhere in Magha the fields turn yellow, and Braj takes it as a signal.

**Vasant Panchami** is the fifth day of the bright fortnight of Magha, kept across India as the first day of spring and as the day of Saraswati. In Braj it is read more narrowly and more happily than that. It is **the day Holi starts**, and the singing that begins this morning does not stop for about forty days.

## When Vasant Panchami falls

| Year | Date | Kept by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **2027** | **Thu 11 February** | Both reckonings |
| **2028** | **Mon 31 January** | Most households |
| 2028 | Tue 1 February | ISKCON and the Gaudiya temples |

Magha shukla panchami. The date moves each year with the tithi and usually lands between late January and late February.

**2028 splits, and by about two minutes.** Panchami begins at 7:14 AM on 31 January, just after sunrise in Delhi. That is enough to fail the udaya tithi test, which asks whether the tithi is running at sunrise, so the Vaishnava calendar moves to 1 February. The general reckoning asks instead whether panchami prevails at **madhyahna**, midday, which it does on 31 January, so most households keep the Monday. Both are right under their own rule. If you are in Braj, keep the day your temple keeps.

## Why Braj begins Holi today

Because the season is not a day here, it is a stretch, and it has to be opened.

On Vasant Panchami the **Holi danda** goes up: a decorated pole planted at **Barsana** and at **Nandgaon**, which stands through the whole season and comes down only at the end. The temples dress their deities in **basanti yellow**. And the **samaj gayan**, the antiphonal singing in which two groups answer each other verse for verse, begins and carries every day of Holi that follows.

At **Banke Bihari in Vrindavan** the first gulal of the year is offered to the deity on this morning. That is the moment the colour is considered to have started. Everything from here to [Lathmar Holi](/festivals/lathmar-holi) at Barsana and on to the [full moon night](/festivals/holi) is one continuous festival, not a run of separate ones.

So when Braj says Holi is forty days, this is day one.

## Why the day is yellow

Two reasons, and they agree with each other.

The first is the ground. **Mustard blooms across north India in Magha**, and for a few weeks the fields between Delhi and Mathura are a flat sheet of yellow. The festival takes its colour from the country it arrives in.

The second is Krishna. Yellow is his colour, the shade of the **pitambar**, the yellow silk he is always shown wearing. To put on basanti on this morning is to dress the way he does at the start of his own season.

So the day is yellow all the way through: yellow cloth, yellow flowers, and yellow food. Kesari bhaat, meethe chawal, boondi laddu, saffron and turmeric in whatever is cooked and offered.

## Kirti Mandir on this day

Barsana holds one more thing on Vasant Panchami that belongs to this site's own lineage.

**Kirti Mandir**, the temple at Barsana dedicated to **Kirti Maiya, Radha's mother**, with Radha enshrined as a child in her lap, keeps Vasant Panchami as one of its festival days. It was founded by [Jagadguru Shri Kripalu Ji Maharaj](/lineage) and is the only temple of its kind anywhere.

There is a fitness to it. The day Braj begins its Holi is kept, in that temple, by a mother and a daughter, at the start of the season that ends with the whole of Braj in colour.

## Saraswati and spring

Across most of India this is **Saraswati Puja**. Books, pens and instruments are placed before her, students ask for her blessing on the year's study, and small children are often given their first letters on this morning, a ceremony called vidyarambham or hate khori.

The two readings of the day sit together without any strain. Vasant Panchami is about something starting: the season, the study, the singing. Braj simply names a different beginning.

## Keeping it at home

Wear yellow. Cook something yellow and offer it. Put yellow flowers on the altar and dress Radha Krishna in yellow if you keep them at home.

If you want to keep Braj's Holi rather than only the two days at the end of it, this is the morning to begin: offer the **first pinch of gulal** at their feet, and let it sit there. Some households then add a little colour to the altar each week until the full moon.

And if there is a child in the house who is about to start school, or an instrument nobody has picked up in a year, this is the traditional morning for both.

## Where this sits in the Braj year

Vasant Panchami opens the season. From here Braj moves through **Laddu Holi** and [Lathmar Holi](/festivals/lathmar-holi) at Barsana and Nandgaon, then the Holi of flowers at Banke Bihari, and finally [Holika Dahan and Rangwali Holi](/festivals/holi) on the Phalguna full moon and the morning after.

The other half of the year belongs to the rains and the autumn: [Jhulan Yatra](/festivals/jhulan-yatra) in Shravana, [Janmashtami](/festivals/janmashtami) and [Radhashtami](/festivals/radhashtami) in Bhadrapada, [Sharad Purnima](/festivals/sharad-purnima) in Ashwin. For the whole cycle, see [the festivals of Radha Krishna](/festivals).

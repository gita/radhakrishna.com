The day after Diwali, Braj does something it has done for five thousand years. It cooks a mountain.

**Govardhan Puja** remembers the week Krishna held a hill over his village. It is kept on Kartik shukla pratipada, and its other name, **Annakut**, means exactly what happens: *anna-kut*, a mountain of food, piled before the deity because the hill itself was once offered a mountain of food.

## When Govardhan Puja falls

| Year | Date |
| --- | --- |
| **2026** | **Tuesday 10 November** |
| 2027 | Saturday 30 October |

The general reckoning and the Vaishnava calendar agree in both years.

You will find sites giving **9 November 2026**, and they are not being careless in the way it looks. Drik Panchang recalculates for the visitor's own city, and for North America it returns the 9th. **For India the date is the 10th.** The reason it can move at all is that pratipada spans two sunrises in 2026, so a classical rule decides: if the moon would rise during the evening puja window, the puja goes to the day before.

## What happened at Govardhan

Every year Vraja held a great sacrifice for **Indra**, the deva of rain. One year Krishna, then seven years old, asked his father why. What followed is one of the plainest arguments in scripture.

The cowherds' living, he said, comes from the cows and the forest and the hill. They are forest people, not townspeople. The rain falls by the working of nature, not as a favour granted. So if a sacrifice is to be made, let it be made to what actually sustains them:

> "Therefore may a sacrifice for the pleasure of the cows, the brāhmaṇas and Govardhana Hill begin! With all the paraphernalia collected for worshiping Indra, let this sacrifice be performed instead."
>
> <cite>Bhagavata Purana 10.24.25</cite>

And then, in the very next verse, he tells them how. Cook everything. Sweet rice, porridge, buns, cakes, every kind of preparation, all the dairy you have. **That verse is where Annakut comes from.**

They did it. They offered to the hill and to the brahmanas with Vedic mantras, **gave grass to the cows**, and then walked all the way around Govardhan with the cattle going in front. That circumambulation is the root of the parikrama devotees still walk.

Then Krishna did something startling. He took an enormous form on the hill itself and declared:

> "Kṛṣṇa then assumed an unprecedented, huge form to instill faith in the cowherd men. Declaring 'I am Govardhana Mountain!' He ate the abundant offerings."
>
> <cite>Bhagavata Purana 10.24.35</cite>

## The lifting

Indra, insulted, sent the samvartaka clouds, the clouds of universal dissolution, and the rain came down on Vraja to destroy it. And a seven-year-old picked up the hill.

> _ity uktvaikena hastena kṛtvā govardhanācalam_
>
> "Having said this, Lord Kṛṣṇa, who is Viṣṇu Himself, picked up Govardhana Hill with one hand and held it aloft just as easily as a child holds up a mushroom."
>
> <cite>Bhagavata Purana 10.25.19</cite>

He held it for **seven days**, without eating or drinking or moving, while the whole village and all its cattle sheltered underneath and looked up at him.

You will often hear that he lifted it on his little finger. That image belongs to Braj devotional idiom and to Pushtimarg iconography, and it is beloved. The verse itself says *ekena hastena*, with one hand, and the Hari-vamsa line quoted alongside it says the left hand.

The story does not end in anyone's defeat. Indra comes back ashamed, admits that pride in his own power blinded him, and mother **Surabhi**, the cow, bathes Krishna with her own milk. Indra bathes him with Ganga water carried in Airavata's trunk and gives him a name that has stayed with him ever since: **Govinda**, the one who pleases the cows and the earth.

## Annakut, the mountain of food

The offering is the festival. A great spread is cooked and arranged before the deity, built up as a hill, and then given out to everyone.

**Chhappan bhog**, the fifty six items, has an arithmetic behind it that people love. Krishna took eight meals a day. He held the hill for seven days and ate nothing. Eight times seven is fifty six, and that is what Vraja cooked him afterwards.

The most famous Annakut in India is at **Shrinathji in Nathdwara**, where a mound of rice representing the hill is raised in the Doltibari with a great sweet gunja set on top, the cows are decorated with peacock plumes and bells at their hooves, and the deity wears a cow-eared crown. Shrinathji himself is the raised **left arm** of Govardhannathji, seen emerging from the hill in 1466 and worshipped ever since, because that is the arm that lifted it.

In Braj the day is kept by circumambulating the hill and offering food to it, a practice the tradition traces to Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.

## Govardhan hill today

It is a sandstone ridge about **8 km long** in Mathura district, standing only about thirty metres above the fields at its highest point. The tradition says it has been shrinking since it came to Braj, and will go on shrinking.

Devotees see the hill as a peacock lying at rest, and name its body: the face at **Kusum Sarovar**, the neck at **Manasi Ganga**, the mouth at **Mukharavind**, the two eyes at **Radha Kund and Shyam Kund**, the tail ending at **Punchari**.

The **parikrama** is about 21 km all the way round, five to seven hours on foot, usually begun at Mansi Ganga and often walked barefoot. It splits into an outer arc of roughly 12 km through Danghati, Anyor and Punchari, and an inner arc of about 9 km past Radha Kund, Shyam Kund and Kusum Sarovar. The path is open at all hours and costs nothing.

**Nobody walks on it.** When Vallabhacharya did the parikrama in 1515 he would not set foot on the hill, holding Govardhan to be non-different from Krishna, and Vaishnavas have not stepped on it since. The ground for it is Krishna's own sentence at 10.24.35. Some devotees perform **dandavati parikrama**, offering full obeisances at every step of the whole 21 km, which takes days.

This is also why a **Govardhan shila**, a stone from the hill, is worshipped as the deity itself. Chaitanya Mahaprabhu gave one to Raghunatha dasa Goswami with the instruction that the stone is the transcendental form of Krishna, and to worship it with great eagerness.

## Keeping it at home

The home version is one of the loveliest in the Hindu year, and it needs nothing you cannot get.

- **Make a Govardhan from cow dung** on the floor or in the courtyard, spread out in the shape of a hill or a reclining figure.
- **Decorate it** with flowers, grass and small twigs for trees, and set little cow figures around it.
- **Light lamps** and walk around it, a parikrama in miniature. This is the oldest part of the observance, straight out of 10.24.32.
- **Cook Annakut** and offer it, as many dishes as your kitchen can manage, then share all of it.
- **Serve the cows.** Wash them, tilak them, garland them and feed them. If there is a goshala near you, this is its day.

Nathdwara does the same thing inside the temple: a mound of cow dung in the Govardhan Puja courtyard, with a stone from the real hill placed on top.

## Other names for the same day

The tithi carries several observances. It is **Annakut** and **Padwa**; in parts of India it is **Bali Pratipada**; and in Gujarat it is **Bestu Varas**, the new year, the first day of the new Vikram Samvat.

## Where this sits in the Radha Krishna year

Govardhan Puja opens Kartik, the month devotees hold dearest, following [Sharad Purnima](/festivals/sharad-purnima) about a fortnight earlier. [Gopashtami](/festivals/gopashtami) comes a week after it. For the whole cycle, see [the festivals of Radha Krishna](/festivals), and for the hill's own country, [Vrindavan](/temples/vrindavan) is about 21 km away.

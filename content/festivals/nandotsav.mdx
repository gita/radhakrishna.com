The night of [Janmashtami](/festivals/janmashtami) is kept quietly. Krishna is born at midnight in a prison at Mathura, the guards asleep, Vasudeva carrying him across a flooded river in the dark. Nobody in Gokul knows yet.

**Nandotsav is the morning after**, when they do. The child is in Nanda Baba's house, the whole of Vraja hears about it, and the celebration that follows is one of the most open-hearted days in the Braj year. Where Janmashtami is a vigil, Nandotsav is drums in daylight.

## When Nandotsav falls

| Year | Date |
| --- | --- |
| **2026** | **Saturday 5 September** |
| 2027 | Thursday 26 August |

Both the general reckoning and the Vaishnava calendar agree in both years, so there is no split to navigate.

One correction worth making plainly, because it is circulating widely. **ISKCON does not keep Janmashtami on 5 September 2026.** ISKCON and the Vaishnava calendar keep Janmashtami on Friday 4 September, the same day as everyone else this year, and Nandotsav on Saturday 5 September. Pages that give 5 September as an "ISKCON Janmashtami" have mistaken Nandotsav for the birth day itself.

The other reason the morning matters: the **parana**, the time to break the Janmashtami fast, falls on Nandotsav morning. The fast ends exactly as the celebration begins.

## What the Bhagavata Purana describes

The whole of it is in **Canto 10, chapter 5**, and the chapter is worth reading straight through on the day. Nanda, overwhelmed with joy, bathes and dresses, calls brahmanas learned in the Vedas, and has the *jata-karma*, the birth ceremony, performed for his son.

Then the giving begins. He gives cows in charity to the brahmanas, decorated with cloth and jewels, and mountains of grain. Vraja itself is dressed: festoons and flags, gateways built of flower garlands and mango leaves, every courtyard swept and washed. The cows and bulls and calves are smeared with turmeric and oil, their heads set with peacock feathers, garlanded and covered in cloth.

The cowherd men arrive in their best, **carrying gifts in their hands**. The gopis, hearing that Yashoda has a son, dress and hurry over with presents, flowers falling from their hair into the street as they go. And then the two verses that the whole day is built on.

> "Offering blessings to the newborn child, Kṛṣṇa, the wives and daughters of the cowherd men said, 'May You become the King of Vraja and long maintain all its inhabitants.' They sprinkled a mixture of turmeric powder, oil and water upon the birthless Supreme Lord and offered their prayers."
>
> <cite>Bhagavata Purana 10.5.12</cite>

> "In gladness, the cowherd men enjoyed the great festival by splashing one another's bodies with a mixture of curd, condensed milk, butter and water. They threw butter on one another and smeared it on one another's bodies."
>
> <cite>Bhagavata Purana 10.5.14</cite>

That verse is why Braj still throws curd and butter and turmeric on Nandotsav. It is not a later custom laid over the text. It is the text.

The chapter closes with Nanda giving clothes, ornaments and cows to the cowherd men, and charity to the reciters and singers and to men of every other profession, **satisfying everyone's desires**. From that time, it says, Nanda's Vraja became the place of the pastimes of the goddess of fortune.

## Why this day is giving rather than fasting

The two days are opposite in shape, and deliberately so.

| | Janmashtami | Nandotsav |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where | Kansa's prison, Mathura | Nanda's open courtyard, Gokul |
| When | Midnight | Daylight |
| How | In secret, in vigil, fasting | In public, with drums, feasting |
| What you do | Wait | Give |

A birth in a prison has to be hidden. A birth in Nanda's house can be shouted about. Chapter 5 is a chapter of giving from beginning to end, and the day copies it.

## Vatsalya, the love that makes God a child

The bhava of Nandotsav is **vatsalya**, loving God as your own son or daughter. It is one of the five primary relationships in Vaishnava devotion, and Nanda and Yashoda are its highest example. As Swami Mukundananda puts it, in vatsalya bhava the devotee takes the role of a parent and God accepts the position of a dependent child.

The Bhagavata does not treat this as a lesser love. When Parikshit asks what Nanda and Yashoda could possibly have done to deserve it, the answer is startling:

> "Neither Lord Brahmā, nor Lord Śiva, nor even the goddess of fortune, who is always the better half of the Supreme Lord, can obtain from the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the deliverer from this material world, such mercy as received by mother Yaśodā."
>
> <cite>Bhagavata Purana 10.9.20</cite>

This same bhava has a home on Radha's side of Braj. [Kirti Mandir at Barsana](/temples/barsana), built by [Jagadguru Shri Kripalu Ji Maharaj](/lineage), is dedicated to **Kirti Maiya, Radha's mother**, with Radha enshrined as a child in her lap. It is the only temple of its kind, and it honours exactly what Nandotsav honours: the love a mother has for her child as its own full path to God.

## How Braj keeps it

**Gokul** carries the day. The child Krishna is brought out from Nand Bhavan and Nand Kila in a **dola**, a palanquin, and carried through the lanes of the bazaar to the Ras Chabutra near Nand Chowk, with shehnai playing and the people of the town showering flowers on it as it passes. Badhai songs are sung. The festivities run from early morning and stretch across three days.

**Nandgaon** keeps it at Nand Bhavan on Nandishwar hill, and does something lovely: **the communities of Nandgaon and Barsana come together** for it, in their best clothes and jewellery. There is group singing and episodes from Krishna's life are enacted, among them Dahi Handi, Mall Yudh, Bans Badhai and Bhanda Leela, and the two villages exchange sweets and gifts. At midnight the deity is bathed in panchamrit inside the sanctum, with only the priests present.

At **Shri Radha Vallabh** in Vrindavan, the temple's Samaji musicians begin singing badhai from before mangala aarti. The deity is bathed in the five substances and then in Yamuna water. The whole temple is dressed in **yellow**, with yellow curtains over the sanctum and banana leaves and kalash set as auspicious signs. Dhaniya barfi, mingi barfi, almond and pistachio barfi and rabri are made for the day, and panchamrit is given at darshan.

At **ISKCON** temples the day carries a second meaning: Srila Prabhupada appeared on Nandotsav in 1896, so it is also his Vyasa Puja.

## The song of the day

One badhai is sung everywhere, and it is the sound of Nandotsav:

> _Nand ke anand bhayo, jai Kanhaiya Lal ki_
>
> "Joy has broken out in Nanda's house. Glory to Nanda's boy."

It is answered with the jaikara *Hathi ghoda palki, jai Kanhaiya Lal ki*. It is a traditional Braj badhai with no single author, and once you have heard a courtyard full of people singing it you will not forget it.

## Keeping Nandotsav at home

Nothing here is difficult, and none of it needs a temple.

- **Break the Janmashtami fast** at the parana time in the morning.
- **Bathe your Laddu Gopal** in panchamrit, the five substances, and dress him in fresh clothes.
- **Sing badhai.** Nand ke anand bhayo is the one.
- **Make sweets and butter** and share them with whoever is near.
- **Give something away.** This is the part that matters most, because it is what Nanda did. Sweets, fruit, clothes, toys, coins. The chapter is a chapter of giving, and the day is a copy of it.

## Where this sits in the Radha Krishna year

Nandotsav follows [Janmashtami](/festivals/janmashtami) by a day, and [Radhashtami](/festivals/radhashtami) comes a fortnight later. Before them, [Jhulan Yatra](/festivals/jhulan-yatra) swings through Shravana, and [Sharad Purnima](/festivals/sharad-purnima) closes the season in October. For the whole cycle, see [the festivals of Radha Krishna](/festivals).

Banke Bihari Temple in Vrindavan houses Thakur Shri Banke Bihari Ji, the deity Swami Haridas received at Nidhivan in the sixteenth century and worshipped as the combined form of Radha and Krishna. The present building was raised in 1862. A curtain closes the darshan every few minutes, no bells are rung inside, and there is no daily morning aarti.

It is the busiest temple in [Vrindavan](/temples/vrindavan) and the one first-time visitors get wrong most often. The doors open late, they shut in the middle of the day, your phone stays outside, and the darshan you queued an hour for lasts a few seconds at a time. All of that is deliberate, and all of it comes from one idea about who lives in the sanctum.

## What does Banke Bihari mean?

Banke means bent. Bihari means one who enjoys, one who revels. Put together they describe Krishna standing bent at three places, weight on one foot, hip thrown out, head tilted, the pose Sanskrit calls tribhanga. It is the pose the Brahma Samhita praises when it worships "the graceful threefold-bending form of Shyamasundara" (5.31, the text Gaudiya Vaishnavas received through Chaitanya Mahaprabhu).

The deity's older name was Kunj Bihari, the one who revels in the groves of Vrindavan. The temple's own invocation still uses it, greeting Kunj Bihari and Kunj Biharini together before it greets Swami Haridas. Devotees mostly drop the formality and say Bihariji.

## Is Banke Bihari both Radha and Krishna?

Yes, and this is the part most guides skip. The image is not Krishna alone with Radha somewhere beside him. It is the two of them in one form.

The temple tells it this way. Swami Haridas sang in a secluded grove at Nidhivan, and his disciples begged to see what he saw there. When they entered they were blinded by light. At Haridas's request the divine couple appeared in person, and everyone present was so held by the sight that they could not blink. Sensing that ordinary people could not carry that much beauty, Haridas asked the two of them to take a single form, like Ghan and Damini, cloud and lightning, the dark Lord and his fair consort. They granted it, and the black image in the sanctum is what they left behind.

So when you hear "Radha Rani ki jai" called out in a temple with one murti in it, that is why. If the theology behind the pairing is new to you, start with [who Radha is](/radha-krishna/who-is-radha) and the wider [Radha Krishna](/radha-krishna) hub.

## Who was Swami Haridas?

A singer, first and last. Haridas composed and sang to reach Krishna, and the tradition that carries his name, the Haridasi sampradaya, still treats music as the practice rather than the decoration around it.

The temple gives his birth as 1478 CE, on Radha Ashtami, and places it at Haridaspur near Aligarh. Other accounts place his birth at Gwalior. He is generally held to have lived to 1573. He left home for Vrindavan, then dense forest, and settled at Nidhivan, which is a few minutes from where the temple stands today.

His lineage is contested in a way worth naming. The Goswami line of his followers traces itself to the Vishnusvami tradition, the sadhu line claims Nimbarka descent, and scholars reading the sources find no evidence that Haridas was initiated into any sect at all. Popular tradition also makes him the teacher of Tansen, which historians consider unlikely on the dates. What is not in dispute is that he was a musician of the first rank in an age full of them, and that Vrindavan calls him the king of rasikas.

Haridas handed the sewa of Bihariji to Goswami Jagannath, his younger brother and principal disciple. The descendants of that family have performed it ever since. The deity stayed near its place of appearance at Nidhivan until a temple worthy of it was built in 1862, paid for by the Goswamis themselves and built in the Rajasthani style of the day. Some accounts give the year as 1864.

## Why is the curtain drawn again and again?

Because darshan here is meant to be broken. A curtain slides across the sanctum, then opens, then closes again, and each brief opening is called a jhanki, a glimpse.

The temple's own explanation is short. The charm of Bihariji is the only reason the darshan is never continuous, and a person who stares long enough into his eyes loses self consciousness. The telling you will hear in the queue goes further. A devotee once held him with such an unbroken gaze that he left the temple and followed her home, and the curtain has been drawn ever since so that no one can hold him that long again.

There is no published interval. Accounts range from a few seconds to a few minutes, and in practice it moves with the crowd and the sewa. Do not plan on a long look. Plan on several short ones, and stand where you can see over the row in front.

## Why are there no bells, no conches and no morning aarti?

Because Bihariji is served as a child, and you do not wake a sleeping child with a bell.

Bells are not hung anywhere inside the temple. Conches are not blown. Instead of the clang most Indian temples run on, you get clapping, singing and the calling of the name. It changes the sound of the place completely, and it is the first thing regular visitors miss at other temples.

The same reasoning explains the late opening. The daily sewa is performed in three parts, shringar, rajbhog and shayan, and there is no mangala sewa at all. Swami Haridas did not favour it. He wanted his child-like Lord to rest and not be roused out of deep sleep so early. The tradition adds that Bihariji is out playing through the night, so the morning belongs to him.

Once a year the rule is set aside. On Janmashtami, and only on Janmashtami, mangala aarti is performed here.

## Why does Banke Bihari not hold a flute?

For most of the year his hands are empty of it. The flute is placed in them on Sharad Purnima, the night of the Maha Raas, and then taken away again. The three-fold bend stays year round, which is the pose that gave him his name.

Note what the table means for a day trip: the doors are shut for four to five hours across the middle of the day, and every other Braj temple keeps a similar gap.

## What happens at Banke Bihari on Janmashtami?

Janmashtami is the one night the temple breaks its own rule. Mangala aarti is performed, the only time in the year it happens here, and darshan runs through the small hours instead of waiting for the usual morning opening.

It is also the heaviest day of the year. Crowds of up to five lakh have been recorded for it, against a daily average of 30,000 to 40,000. Two devotees died of suffocation in a crush at the temple on Janmashtami in 2022, and the Supreme Court later pointed to that day when it took up the question of infrastructure around the temple. A corridor project to create holding space for the crowd has been through the courts since, along with a dispute over how the temple is administered. The sewa inside the sanctum is unaffected by any of it.

Nandotsav, the celebration of the birth in Nanda's house, follows the next day. For the festival itself, its date and how it is kept across Braj, see [Janmashtami](/festivals/janmashtami).

## What is Holi like at Banke Bihari?

Long. Braj ki Holi does not start on the day the rest of India plays it, and at this temple it opens on Amla Ekadashi, also called Rangbhari Ekadashi, and runs until Dhulendi.

The signature is phoolon wali Holi. On Rangbhari Ekadashi the Goswamis shower flowers from the sanctum over the crowd instead of colour, and the hall fills with petals for a few minutes of complete abandon. Colour play builds from there, Holika Dahan closes the colour days, and Dhulendi is kept as Dolotsav. Barsana's Lathmar Holi runs a few days before all of this and is worth planning around; it is covered on [Barsana](/temples/barsana).

Radha Ashtami carrying two birthdays is not a coincidence the temple treats lightly. Haridas is held to have been born on the same tithi as Radharani, which is [Radhashtami](/festivals/radhashtami), and the tradition reads him as an incarnation of Lalita, the closest of Radha's sakhis.

## How should you plan a visit?

Go early on a weekday. That single choice does more for your darshan than anything else.

The temple names its own worst days: Ekadashi, Purnima and Amavasya every month, plus weekends, public holidays and the annual festivals. It advises elderly devotees and people with small children to stay away on festival days outright. Wheelchair assistance is available near Sneh Bihari Mandir, and the sewa adhikaris arrange help for anyone who needs it.

Three things catch visitors out:

1. **There is no VIP darshan.** The temple says so in plain words on its own site, and there is no online booking. Inside the temple you can buy a ticket for the first row. Everything offered to you outside the gate at a thousand rupees is somebody's side business.
2. **Leave your phone behind.** Mobile phones, electronic gadgets, leather items and umbrellas are all on the temple's list of things not to carry in. Footwear stays outside. So does photography, and the security staff enforce it.
3. **Registration is not required.** It was once, and stale pages still say so. It is not needed now.

Getting there is simple. Mathura Junction is the railway station to book, about 14 km away, with taxis, autos and buses running into Vrindavan. From Delhi it is the Yamuna Expressway or NH 44, roughly 150 km. Agra airport is about 70 km, Delhi about 150 km. Inside Vrindavan the last stretch is on foot through narrow lanes, and any Brajwasi will point you the rest of the way.

Bathe and wear clean clothes before you go, dress modestly, and watch for pickpockets and monkeys in the lanes. Offerings go into the hundi or the branch office inside the premises, not into anybody's hand.

## Where to go next

Nidhivan, where all of this began, is a short walk away and closes at dusk for reasons the town takes seriously. The rest of the town, the seven historic Goswami temples and the ghats, is mapped on [Vrindavan](/temples/vrindavan), and the wider region on the [Temples and Braj](/temples) hub. Radha's own town is [Barsana](/temples/barsana), and her appearance there is told in the [birth of Radharani](/stories/birth-of-radharani).

Carry a prayer with you. The [Hare Krishna maha mantra](/mantras/hare-krishna-maha-mantra) is the one you will hear on every lane in Vrindavan. And if the dark image in the sanctum makes you wonder about the colour at all, that question has its own page: [why is Krishna blue](/questions/why-is-krishna-blue).

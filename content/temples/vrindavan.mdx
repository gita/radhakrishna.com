Vrindavan is a temple town on the west bank of the Yamuna in Mathura district, Uttar Pradesh, about 15 km north of Mathura. Hindus hold it to be where Krishna spent his childhood and youth with Radha and the gopis. Roughly 5,500 temples stand in it, from 16th century Goswami shrines to modern marble complexes, and pilgrims walk a 10 km circuit around the whole town.

It is small. The 2011 census counted just over 63,000 residents, and you can cross the old quarter on foot in twenty minutes. What it holds is out of all proportion to its size, which is why a first visit can feel overwhelming. This page is the ground map: what is here, what each place is for, and what to do about the crowds. For the wider region, start at [temples and Braj](/temples).

## What is Vrindavan famous for?

For being the setting of almost every Krishna story people love.

The name is literal. Vrindavan comes from vrinda, holy basil, and vana, grove. This was forest before it was a town, and the tradition still calls it a van rather than a city. In the Bhagavata Purana, Nanda and the cowherds abandon Gokul and move the whole settlement here in Canto 10, chapter 11, after a run of attacks on the infant Krishna. Everything that follows, the cows, the flute, the butter, the gopis, happens on this ground.

The peak of it is the raas, the circle dance told across Bhagavata Purana 10.29 to 10.33. Radha is never named in that text, and the tradition that names her builds on later sources, which [who Radha is](/radha-krishna/who-is-radha) works through carefully.

The other half of Vrindavan's fame is what it lost. In Bhagavata Purana 10.39, Akrura drives Krishna and Balarama away to Mathura, and Vrindavan never gets him back. Chapter 47, the Song of the Bee, has Uddhava returning to a town of women who will not be consoled. This is the place of both the meeting and the separation, and Braj devotion leans harder on the second.

Every conversation in the town starts with "Radhe Radhe". Not hello, not namaste. Her name.

## Where is Vrindavan, and how is it different from Mathura?

They are two different towns 15 km apart, and confusing them is the single most common mistake visitors make.

| | Mathura | Vrindavan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What happened here | Krishna was born in Kansa's prison | Krishna grew up, played the flute, and met Radha |
| Feel | A working district headquarters with a temple core | A dense temple town of narrow lanes |
| The main sites | Krishna Janmabhoomi, Dwarkadhish temple, Vishram Ghat | Banke Bihari, the Goswami temples, Nidhivan, Prem Mandir, Keshi Ghat |
| Getting there | Mathura Junction is the railhead for both | 15 km north of Mathura, about 30 minutes by road |
| How long you need | Half a day | A full day at minimum, two days comfortably |

Almost everyone arrives through Mathura Junction, then takes an auto, taxi or shared tempo north. Vrindavan had its own station on a branch line from Mathura, opened in 1889, but it closed in 2023 and the track has been lifted, so the junction is now the only realistic rail option. By road from Delhi it is a three to three and a half hour run on the Yamuna Expressway. The nearest airport of any size is Agra, and most people simply drive.

## Why did Vrindavan have to be rediscovered?

Because for a long stretch nobody could say for certain where anything was. The forest had swallowed the sites, and the local memory of which grove was which had thinned.

Chaitanya Mahaprabhu came to Vrindavan in 1515 for exactly that reason, to find the lost places. He did not stay. He sent others, the scholars remembered as the Six Goswamis, with a standing brief: locate the sites, install deities, write the books, establish the worship. Almost every historic temple in the town traces to that project or to the patrons who funded it.

He was not alone in that century. Vallabhacharya had already been coming and staying for months at a time, and Vrindavan shaped his Pushtimarg. Hit Harivansh founded the Radha Vallabha Sampradaya here in 1535, a school that worships Radha first and Krishna as hers. Swami Haridas, the dhrupad musician, did his bhajan in the grove now called Nidhivan, and the deity of Banke Bihari is said to have appeared to him there.

Four distinct lineages put down roots in the same small town within a few decades, and their temples still keep four different styles of worship a few lanes apart. Vrindavan is not one tradition. It is several, standing very close together.

## Which temples should you actually visit?

There are about 5,500. These are the ones that carry the town.

| Temple | What it is | Known for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Banke Bihari | The busiest temple in Vrindavan; deity brought from Nidhivan, present building around 1864 | The curtain drawn across the deity every few minutes; no bells anywhere; the flute held only on Sharad Purnima |
| Radha Raman | Established by Gopala Bhatta Goswami, 1542 | The deity self-manifested from a shaligram shila. There is no separate Radha murti, only her crown beside him |
| Radha Vallabh | Seat of the Radha Vallabha Sampradaya; the old temple completed 1585 | Radha is present as a crown on the seat, not an image. Her name is sung before his, always |
| Nidhivan | A grove, not a built temple | The bhajan place of Swami Haridas, where Banke Bihari appeared. Locked and barricaded after dark |
| Prem Mandir | Established and inaugurated by Jagadguru Shri Kripalu Ji Maharaj in February 2012 | White marble carved with the lilas, on a 54 acre site. Lit up and musical in the evening |
| Krishna Balaram Mandir (ISKCON) | Deities installed by Srila Prabhupada in 1975, in Raman Reti | Prabhupada's white marble samadhi adjoins it; the best organised darshan in town for foreign visitors |

Two more reward the detour. Radha Damodar and Madan Mohan sit in the old quarter and hold the Goswami samadhis, so they are where the 16th century is thickest. On the outskirts, the Vrindavan Chandrodaya Mandir is still under construction and is planned as the tallest religious monument in the world.

If you only have one morning, give it to [Banke Bihari](/temples/banke-bihari) and go early. That temple is now run by a Supreme Court appointed committee, which scrapped VIP and paid queue-jumping access and extended the public darshan windows. Anyone offering to sell you special darshan is selling something they do not have.

## What is the mystery of Nidhivan?

Nidhivan is a walled grove of short tulsi trees whose trunks twist downward and grow in pairs. The town's belief is that they are the gopis, and that they take their own form at night.

Inside the grove stands the Rang Mahal. Each evening the priests lay out a bed, ornaments, water, neem twigs for cleaning the teeth, and betel leaf and sweets. Then the gates are locked from outside and the whole grove is barricaded. In the morning the bed looks slept in, the twigs used, the sweets partly eaten. Nobody stays inside. The houses that overlook the grove keep their upper floors shut after dark.

The belief is that Radha and Krishna come back here for the raas every night, and that a person who watches does not come away with their sight, speech or mind intact.

No scripture records any of this. There is no verse, no chapter, no Purana that describes the nightly return to Nidhivan. It is living tradition of Vrindavan itself, held with total conviction by people who have kept the grove for generations. That is a different kind of claim from a scriptural one, and it is worth being clear about which is which, because the two get blurred constantly online.

## What is left of the Yamuna at Vrindavan?

Less than the paintings suggest, and more than the complaints do.

Keshi Ghat is the main one, a run of sandstone steps and old facades below the Madan Mohan temple. It is named for the horse demon Keshi, killed by Krishna in Bhagavata Purana 10.37, after which he bathed here. Chir Ghat, a little west, is where the gopis' clothes were taken while they bathed, the episode retold in [the day Krishna dressed as a gopi](/stories/when-krishna-dressed-as-a-gopi). An evening aarti is done on the steps and boats work the stretch in front.

Be realistic about the water. The Yamuna reaches Braj carrying most of Delhi's load, the channel has shifted away from parts of the old bank, and cleanup schemes have run for decades without fixing it. People still bathe. Many visitors touch the water and leave it there. Go at sunrise or for the evening aarti, when the ghat is at its best.

## How long is the Vrindavan parikrama?

About 10 km, and two to three hours if you walk it.

A parikrama is a circumambulation, a walk around something holy keeping it on your right. In Braj it counts for more than any single darshan. The Vrindavan circuit runs around the whole town on the Parikrama Marg, passes close to the ISKCON temple, and is walked most heavily on Ekadashi. Many people do it barefoot. E-rickshaws run the loop in under an hour for anyone who cannot.

| Circuit | Distance | Time on foot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vrindavan parikrama | about 10 km | 2 to 3 hours |
| Govardhan parikrama | 21 km | 5 to 7 hours |
| Braj 84 kos yatra | roughly 252 km | Days to weeks, with village halts |

None of them require a booking, a guide or a fee. They require time and feet you are willing to punish.

## When should you go, and what lands in Vrindavan?

October to March. Days are pleasant, evenings are cool, and the biggest festivals sit inside that window or just outside it. May and June are brutal; the monsoon is green but heavy going in the lanes.

| Festival | When | What Vrindavan does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Holi | Feb to Mar, over several weeks | The whole Braj season, with Lathmar Holi at [Barsana](/temples/barsana) first, then colour through Vrindavan and Mathura |
| Jhulan Yatra | Shravana, Jul to Aug | Radha and Krishna on decorated swings through the monsoon |
| [Janmashtami](/festivals/janmashtami) | Bhadrapada, Aug to Sep | Krishna's birth kept at midnight; the town does not sleep |
| [Radhashtami](/festivals/radhashtami) | 15 days later | Radha's appearance day; Barsana is bigger, Vrindavan is fuller |
| Sharad Purnima | Ashwin full moon, Sep to Oct | The raas night. Banke Bihari holds the flute this one day of the year |
| Kartik | Oct to Nov | The most auspicious month here. Lamps on the ghats, parikramas every day |

Whatever you pick, plan around the midday shutdown. Almost every temple in Vrindavan opens early, closes for a few hours around noon while the deities are bathed, fed and rested, then reopens in the late afternoon until the evening aarti. First-timers lose an entire day to this. Temples in the morning, ghats and gardens and prasad in the afternoon, aarti at dusk. The full year is laid out in [festivals](/festivals).

## What nobody tells you before you go

The town is holy. It is also crowded, and it has learned to work visitors.

Monkeys take spectacles and phones on purpose, especially around Banke Bihari and Nidhivan, and they trade them back for fruit or juice that someone conveniently sells nearby. Keep glasses in a case and phones out of sight. Do not chase.

Autos will quote several times the fare and drop you at a shop that is not where you asked to go. Fix the price before you sit. Temples have asked visitors to dress modestly, and the queue at Banke Bihari on a festival day is genuinely crushing, so go at opening or skip the festival dates entirely if you are with elderly parents or small children.

There is a harder thing here too. Vrindavan has for generations drawn widows, many abandoned by their families, who live in ashrams and bhajan halls and sing for hours a day in exchange for shelter and a meal. The National Commission for Women has studied the conditions; state and NGO homes have improved parts of it and not all of it. If you visit, give to a run home rather than on the street, and do not photograph anyone without asking.

None of this cancels the place. It sits alongside it, which is what a living pilgrimage town looks like.

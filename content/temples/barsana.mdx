Barsana is a hill town in Mathura district, Uttar Pradesh, about 45 km from both Mathura and Vrindavan. Braj knows it as Radha's town, the seat of her father Vrishabhanu and the home of the Shriji temple on Bhanugarh hill. It is where Lathmar Holi begins each spring and where Radhashtami is kept most grandly.

Vrindavan belongs to Krishna. Barsana belongs to her. You hear it in the greeting on the street, which is not Hare Krishna but Radhe Radhe, and you see it in the temple name: Ladli Ji, the beloved daughter of the town. This page covers the hill, the temple, the birthplace question that devotees argue about, the two festivals worth planning a year around, and what a visit actually looks like. Start from [Temples and Braj](/temples) for the wider map.

## Where is Barsana, and is it in Vrindavan or Mathura?

Barsana is its own town, a nagar panchayat in Mathura district at 27.65 N, 77.38 E. It is not a neighbourhood of either Mathura or Vrindavan, though all three sit inside Braj, the cultural region that holds Krishna's childhood geography.

The distances are short but the roads are rural, so budget more time than the kilometres suggest.

| From | Distance by road | Usual driving time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vrindavan | about 45 km | 1 to 1.5 hours |
| Mathura | about 45 to 50 km | 1 to 1.5 hours |
| Govardhan | about 20 km | 30 to 40 minutes |
| Nandgaon | about 10 km | 20 to 30 minutes |

The town is also called Vrishabhanupur, after Radha's father. Local saints read the name Barsana from barasna, to rain or shower, and preach it as the place where her mercy showers down. That is how the name is heard in Braj rather than a settled linguistic derivation.

## Why is Barsana called Radha's birthplace, and what about Raval?

This is the single most argued question about Barsana, and the honest answer is that two traditions run side by side.

The case for Barsana rests on Vrishabhanu. The Padma Purana describes him as the Yadava chief of Barsana, master of a vast herd of cows. If her father's seat was Barsana, her home was Barsana, and the town has held her deity and her festival for centuries.

The case for Raval rests on a line of text. The Garga Samhita, the scripture that carries the fullest account of her appearance, says at 1.8.6 that Radha descended into a great palace in a grove on the Yamuna's shore. Raval is a village on the north bank of the Yamuna about 5 km from Gokul, and it holds the Radha Janmasthali temple that marks the spot. Barsana stands roughly 45 km inland, with no river. The geography is the argument.

| Claim | What stands behind it | What kind of source it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Radha was born at Barsana | The Padma Purana names Vrishabhanu as chief of Barsana; the town's unbroken worship, the Shriji temple, and Uttar Pradesh's own tourism record | Purana plus living Braj tradition |
| Radha was born at Raval and grew up at Barsana | Garga Samhita 1.8.6 places her descent in a palace in a grove on the Yamuna's bank, which matches Raval's location; widely held in Gaudiya Vaishnava guidebooks and pilgrimage practice | Named scripture plus Gaudiya tradition |
| Both, in sequence | Braj tradition holds that Vrishabhanu shifted his household from Raval to Barsana in her childhood, around the time Nanda Maharaj moved to Nandgaon | Devotional tradition, not a chapter and verse |

Notice what the disagreement is not about. Nobody disputes that Barsana is where she grew up, where her sakhis lived, and where her pastimes with Krishna are set. The reconciliation most devotees hold, that Raval is the appearance place and Barsana the home, keeps both claims intact. For how the birth itself is told text by text, read [the birth of Srimati Radharani](/stories/birth-of-radharani).

## What is the Shriji temple, and who built it?

The Shriji temple, known locally as Ladli Ji Maharaj, crowns Bhanugarh hill above the town. The deities are Radha and Krishna worshipped together as Shri Ladli Lal, the beloved daughter and the beloved son. Idols of Vrishabhanu, Kirtida and Shridama stand at the base of the palace, which is the point: this is a family home before it is a shrine.

Braj tradition traces the original deity to Vajranabha, Krishna's great-grandson, and holds that the site was lost over the centuries. Narayan Bhatt, the sixteenth-century saint who spent his life identifying the forgotten places of Braj, is credited with recovering it. The present structure of red and white sandstone, with its arches, carved pillars and painted domes in the Rajput manner, is dated to 1675 and linked to Raja Bir Singh Deo.

Inside, the darshan is close and loud. Barsana is a singing town, and the temple runs on samaj gayan, the call and response of Braj devotional song, more than on silence.

## How many steps are there, and is there a ropeway?

More than 200 steps climb from the town to the temple gate. They are wide, worn and lined with shops, monkeys and people who will offer to carry your shoes.

A ropeway opened on 25 August 2024, timed to Janmashtami, and lifts pilgrims to the temple in about three minutes. It has made the darshan possible for elderly devotees who had given up on the climb. Most people still walk up and ride down, or the reverse.

Keep your phone and your glasses zipped away on the stairs. The monkeys of Barsana are practised and organised, and they trade what they take for a packet of biscuits.

## What are the four hills of Barsana?

Four peaks ring the town: Bhanugarh, Mangarh, Dangarh and Vilasgarh. Braj tradition, drawing on the Padma and Varaha Purana accounts, holds that Brahma asked to receive the dust of Radha and Krishna's feet and was granted the form of these hills, which is why the ridge is called Brahmagiri or Brahmachal and its peaks are read as his four faces.

Each hill carries a pastime, which is how the town is walked.

| Place | Hill or setting | What the tradition remembers here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shriji temple (Ladli Ji) | Bhanugarh | Radha's palace and the main darshan of the town |
| Maan Mandir | Mangarh | Where Radha withdrew in mana, loving anger, with a cave below where she is said to have hidden |
| Kushal Bihari (Jaipur Mandir) | Dangarh | The dan lila, Krishna demanding a toll from the gopis |
| Mor Kutir | near Vilasgarh | Radha and Krishna dancing in the form of peahen and peacock |
| Sankari Khor | the cleft between two hills | The narrow pass where Krishna blocked the gopis carrying milk to market |
| Gahvar van and Radha Sarovar | the grove below the hills | The forest of caves where Radha bathed and played |
| Pili Pokhar | town edge | The pond turned yellow when Kirtida washed the turmeric from her hands |
| Kirti Mandir | Rangeeli Mahal | Founded by Jagadguru Shri Kripalu Ji Maharaj and inaugurated on Basant Panchami in February 2019, showing Radha as a child in her mother's lap |
| Prem Sarovar | Barsana to Nandgaon road | The lake said to have formed from the tears of Radha and Krishna |

The Barsana parikrama, a walk of roughly 5 km, strings these together. Done unhurried with darshan at each stop, it fills a morning.

## Why is Lathmar Holi played between Barsana and Nandgaon?

Because Holi in Braj is a story about two villages, not one festival.

The episode behind it is simple. Krishna came over from Nandgaon with his friends to smear colour on Radha and her sakhis. The women of Barsana were ready, and they chased the boys out with sticks. Every spring the two towns play it again in earnest.

Men come from Nandgaon to Barsana on Phalguna Shukla Navami and gather in Rangili Gali, the lane below the Shriji temple. The women of Barsana meet them with long bamboo lathis. The men take the blows on thick leather shields, sing back, and hold their ground as long as they can. The next day, Dashami, Barsana's men go to Nandgaon and the women there return the favour.

| Day | Where | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phalguna Shukla Ashtami | Barsana | Laddu Holi. The invitation carried over from Nandgaon is accepted and laddus are showered over the crowd |
| Phalguna Shukla Navami | Barsana, Rangili Gali | Lathmar Holi. Men from Nandgaon play colour, the women answer with lathis |
| Phalguna Shukla Dashami | Nandgaon | Lathmar Holi again, with the roles reversed |

All of this sits inside a much longer season. Braj Holi runs about forty days from Basant Panchami, with temple rituals, Holi gayan and gulal through the whole stretch, and Lathmar Holi lands roughly a week to nine days before the Holi the rest of India keeps. If you are travelling for it, book months ahead and expect crowds that fill the lanes wall to wall. There are also years when the crush and the behaviour of some visitors have drawn real criticism, so go with a group and keep to the temple-side of the celebrations.

## What happens at Barsana on Radhashtami?

Radhashtami, the eighth day of the bright fortnight of Bhadrapada, is her appearance day and the biggest day of the Barsana year. It falls about a fortnight after [Janmashtami](/festivals/janmashtami).

The Shriji temple bathes the deities in a long abhishek, dresses them in new finery, and keeps darshan open far beyond the usual hours. The town fills with kirtan parties, flower showers, and queues that wind down the hill and through the lanes. Many devotees fast until noon and take charnamrit and prasad afterwards. On this one day, the deity is customarily brought where the crowd can see her without the usual distance.

If you want one day in Barsana in your life, this is the day, and it is also the hardest day to move around in. The full observance and this year's date are on [Radhashtami](/festivals/radhashtami).

## When should you go, and what should you know first?

October to March is the comfortable season, and it holds Radhashtami at its start and Braj ki Holi at its end. Summer in Braj is brutal on an exposed hill with stone steps.

Published timings put the morning darshan from about 5 am until midday and the evening session from around 5 pm to the evening aarti. They shift with the season and change again during festivals, so check on the day rather than planning around a number. The long midday closing is when the deities are bathed, fed and rested, and it catches out almost every first-time visitor.

Practical notes worth having. There is no entry fee. Dress modestly and be ready to leave shoes at the bottom of the stairs. Photography is restricted inside the sanctum. Barsana pairs naturally with Nandgaon 10 km away, and the two make one unhurried day out of Vrindavan or Mathura, best done in that order so you arrive at Radha's town first.

Walk anywhere in Braj and you will hear it. Not hello, not goodbye, not good morning. **Radhe Radhe.** Shopkeepers say it, rickshaw drivers say it, children say it, and the old man sweeping the ghat steps says it to nobody in particular. It is painted on walls, on the trunks of trees and on the backs of trucks.

It is the shortest and most complete thing this tradition says.

## What Radhe Radhe means

It means **O Radha, O Radha.**

The name is Radha. **Radhe** is the vocative, the form Sanskrit uses for addressing someone directly rather than talking about them. So the greeting is not a description or a claim. It is a call.

That distinction is worth holding onto, because it answers the question people ask most often. Say **Radha** when you speak *about* her. Say **Radhe** when you speak *to* her. A greeting is speech directed at her, which is why Braj says Radhe.

And when two people meet, neither of them tells the other anything at all. They call her name together, and go on their way.

## The name holds two opposite meanings

Here is where it becomes remarkable, and where our lineage says something most explanations leave out.

> Radha is a root word in Sanskrit language. Its meaning is aradhana (worship) and upasna (worship). The word Radha has two meanings and both are correct. One meaning of Radha is the one who prays and worships Shri Krishn. The other meaning is, the one Who is worshipped by Brahm Shri Krishn is Radha. So, we have two opposite meanings. She is called Radha because She worships Brahm Krishn, and She is called Radha because Brahm Krishn worships Her.
>
> <cite>Jagadguru Shri Kripalu Ji Maharaj, Radhashtami lecture "Who is Shri Radha?"</cite>

Both halves have a text behind them.

**She who worships** comes from the Bhagavata Purana. When Krishna slips away from the rasa dance with one gopi, the others find her footprints and say *anaya aradhitah*, this one must have worshipped him perfectly. The Gaudiya acharyas derive her name from that word: *"The name Radha is derived from the root word aradhana, which means worship. The personality who excels all in worshiping Krsna may therefore be called Radhika"* (Chaitanya Charitamrita, Adi 4.87).

**She whom he worships** comes from the Brahma Vaivarta Purana, where Krishna himself says to her: O Kishori Ji, please forgive my mistakes and grace me.

Both are in scripture. Neither cancels the other. That is the name.

## A third meaning: she who fulfils every desire

The Devi Bhagavata Purana gives another root, and this one is the plainest of all:

> _radhnoti sakalan kamams tasmad radheti kirtita_
>
> "The name Râdhâ is derived from 'Râdhnoti' or fulfills all desires. Hence Mûla Prakriti is termed Râdhâ."
>
> <cite>Devi Bhagavata Purana 9.50.18, translated by Swami Vijnanananda</cite>

Kripalu Ji Maharaj gave the same meaning with a detail that is hard to forget: she is the one who fulfils all the desires of all the souls when they shed a few tears.

## Why Braj calls her name rather than his

There is a story Braj tells about this. It is devotional tradition rather than a numbered verse, and it is the warmest answer anyone gives.

Radha taught her parrot to say *Krishna, Krishna*. The other parrots picked it up, then her sakhis, and soon the whole of Vrindavan was chanting Krishna's name, and nobody was chanting hers. Krishna grew sad, because the sound he loves best is her name. So Radha, without a moment's thought for how it would look, set about spreading her own name through Braj, purely so that he would be happy.

That is why the lanes say Radhe.

Our own lineage puts the same principle without the story. Kripalu Ji Maharaj: *first you say Radha's name, then Krishna's.* Nobody says Krishna Radha. It is Radha Krishna, Shyama Shyam, Radhe Govind. Her name goes first.

## What our lineage teaches about this name

The teacher this site follows spent his life on this one word. Jagadguru Kripalu Parishat records the three things he gave everyone he met:

> Shree Radha is the ultimate goal of every living being.
>
> Shree Radha is the essence of all scriptures.
>
> Shree Radha is the personification of the highest state of divine love known as mahabhava.
>
> <cite>Jagadguru Kripalu Parishat</cite>

His **Radha Govind Geet** runs to 11,111 couplets, and it opens with the greeting itself:

> _Radhe Radhe Govind Govind Radhe_
> _Radha Krishna Naam Hai Govind Radhe_
>
> <cite>Radha Govind Geet, opening couplets</cite>

The instruction he gave for it is not a count. It is a continuity:

> "During all your activities of daily life such as eating, drinking or walking, always keep chanting the sweet name of Shri Radha. Don't ever forget the name of Shri Radhe, not even for a fraction of a second."
>
> <cite>Radha Govind Geet 6597</cite>

And in his last years he did it himself in the simplest possible way. He would rise at midnight, come out of his room for a walk, and **call out "Radhe"** across the assembled crowd, who would call it joyfully back. Often a new verse in praise of her name came out of him on the spot.

## The one condition

This is the part that is usually left off, and our lineage never leaves it off.

> As per the teachings of Shri Maharaj Ji, while reciting the name of the Lord, two things are mandatory, remembrance of the Lord's form and love for the Lord. Without such loving remembrance and meditation on the form of God, one will derive no benefit even if you chant Radhe Radhe all your life.
>
> <cite>Jagadguru Kripalu Parishat</cite>

Swami Mukundananda calls that remembrance **roopdhyan**, and says of it that without roopdhyan, kirtan is a beautiful body without breath. The name is not a machine. It is an address, and someone has to be meant by it.

## And nobody is shut out of it

Worth saying plainly, because people ask whether they are allowed to.

> Anyone can take any name of God and recite it freely, if by doing so his attachment and devotion to God is enhanced. It could be Radhe Govind, Hare Rama Hare Krishna or Radhe Shyam. The Supreme Lord is one and it is transgression to differentiate between any names of God, be it Rama or Shyam.
>
> <cite>Jagadguru Kripalu Parishat</cite>

No initiation is needed. No qualification, no background, no permission. Braj greets whoever arrives.

## The other greetings you will hear

| What you hear | What it means | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Radhe Radhe** | O Radha, O Radha | The everyday greeting across Braj |
| **Jai Shri Radhe** | Glories to Radha | Often in temples |
| **Radhe Shyam** | Radha and the dark beautiful one | Common, and one of the chants our lineage recommends |
| **Radhe Krishna** | Their two names together | Widely used, and the core of the Nimbarka chant |
| **Radhe Govind** | Radha and Govind | Chanted at JKP centres on the morning parikrama |
| **Jai Shri Krishna** | Glories to Krishna | Widely used beyond Braj, especially in Gujarat |
| **Hare Krishna** | The [maha mantra](/mantras/hare-krishna-maha-mantra) as greeting | The Gaudiya and ISKCON form |

Nobody in Braj will correct you for choosing one over another.

**Where it came from is not recorded.** No source names a century, a saint or a moment when Braj began greeting this way. All anyone can say is that Braj has kept it, and keeps it still.

## How to chant it

**Start small and constant rather than long and occasional.** JKYog's guidance for beginners is to say it softly eleven times in the morning, then attach it to ordinary things: while the water boils, while locking the door, while starting the car. When something is difficult, one *Radhe* under the breath.

**On a mala** the method is the ordinary one: 108 beads and a Meru bead, held in the right hand between thumb and middle finger rather than the index finger, one Radhe Radhe per bead, and at the end of a round you turn back rather than crossing the Meru.

**In kirtan** it is sung rather than counted, and our lineage treats that as threefold devotion at once: chanting it, hearing it, and holding their form in mind while you do.

But the instruction underneath all of these is the one above: through eating, drinking and walking, and never forgotten for a fraction of a second.

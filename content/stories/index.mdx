The stories of Radha and Krishna are called lilas, the Sanskrit word for play. Most of Krishna's Vrindavan pastimes, from Putana to the Raas Lila, are told in the tenth canto of the Bhagavata Purana. Radha is never named there. Her stories come from later texts like the Garga Samhita and the Brahma Vaivarta Purana, and from Jayadeva's Gita Govinda.

That last part surprises almost everyone, and it is the honest place to begin. This page lays out what each of the great lilas is, which text actually carries it, and where to go next.

**Key takeaways**

- Lila means play. The word is a claim about why Krishna acts, not only about what he does.
- The Bhagavata Purana, Canto 10, holds most of the famous pastimes, each with a chapter you can look up.
- Radha is absent by name from both the Bhagavata Purana and the Bhagavad Gita.
- She is named and central in the Brahma Vaivarta Purana, the Garga Samhita, and Jayadeva's 12th century Gita Govinda.
- Braj still keeps the stories in place and in season, at Barsana, at Govardhan, and on Sharad Purnima.

## What does lila mean, and why are these called lilas?

Lila, also spelled leela, is Sanskrit for play or sport. Shankara used the image of a king engaged in a game. The act is deliberate and conscious, and there is no ulterior motive behind it. That is the difference the word is pointing at. Krishna does not lift a hill because he needs to. He lifts it the way a child picks up a mushroom, and the tradition reads the ease itself as the teaching.

So a lila is a story with a claim inside it. Read it as an anecdote and it is charming. Read it as lila and it is saying something about how God relates to the world.

## Where do these stories actually come from?

Four bodies of text carry nearly all of it, and they do not say the same things.

| Text | What it gives you | Is Radha named? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bhagavata Purana, Canto 10 | The childhood lilas, Govardhan, the Raas Lila, Dwarka, the queens | No |
| Brahma Vaivarta Purana | Radha as Krishna's eternal other half, in her own right | Yes |
| Garga Samhita | Radha's birth and the Braj pastimes in devotional detail | Yes |
| Gita Govinda, Jayadeva, 12th century | Radha as the named beloved, union and separation as poetry | Yes |

Read only the Bhagavata and you get Krishna without Radha. Read only the Gita Govinda and you get the love without the cosmology. Most devotees hold both. That is why our pages tell you which text a detail comes from instead of blending them into one smooth story.

## Why is Radha missing from the Bhagavata Purana?

She is not named in it once. The nearest thing is verse 10.30.28, where the gopis notice Krishna has left them all and taken one gopi aside, and say she must have worshipped him perfectly. Gaudiya commentators read that unnamed gopi as Radha. Others do not. It is one of the genuinely contested points in the tradition, so we label it instead of smoothing it over.

Her theology fills in later, through the Puranas that name her and through Jayadeva. For the fuller picture, read [who Radha is](/radha-krishna/who-is-radha) and [what their love symbolizes](/radha-krishna/what-their-love-symbolizes).

## How do these stories live on in Braj today?

Braj remembers by geography and by calendar. Barsana is Radha's town, Nandgaon is the village of Nanda Maharaj where Krishna spent his boyhood, Govardhan is the hill itself, and Vrindavan is where the Raas is set. You can walk all of them in a few days. Our [temples and Braj](/temples) pages cover what each place is known for.

The calendar carries the rest. Janmashtami falls on the eighth day of the dark fortnight of Bhadrapada, Radhashtami on the eighth day of the bright fortnight of that same month, and Sharad Purnima, the autumn full moon of Ashwin, is kept as Raas Purnima, the night of the Maha Raas. See [festivals](/festivals) for the year.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does lila (leela) mean?

Lila is Sanskrit for play or sport. In Vedantic thought it means an act done out of fullness rather than need. Shankara used the image of a king playing a game: the act is deliberate, and there is no ulterior motive behind it. That is why Krishna's deeds are called lilas and not simply events.

### Which book should I read for the complete Radha Krishna story?

There is no single book, which is why the question comes up so often. Start with the Bhagavata Purana, Canto 10, for Krishna's childhood, Govardhan and the Raas Lila. Then read Jayadeva's Gita Govinda for Radha as the named beloved. The Garga Samhita and the Brahma Vaivarta Purana fill in her birth and her place beside him. Braj poets like Surdas carry the rest in Braj Bhasha.

### Is Radha mentioned in the Bhagavata Purana or the Bhagavad Gita?

Not by name in either. The Bhagavad Gita is Krishna's teaching to Arjuna at Kurukshetra and has no Vrindavan narrative in it at all. The Bhagavata Purana tells the Vrindavan lilas but never names Radha. Gaudiya commentators read verse 10.30.28, about the one gopi Krishna took aside, as an indirect reference to her. Other readers do not.

### What is the Raas Lila, and where is it in scripture?

The Raas Lila is Krishna's autumn night dance with the gopis of Vrindavan. It is told in the Bhagavata Purana, Canto 10, chapters 29 to 33, the five chapters known as the rasa panchadhyayi. Chapter 31 is the Gopi Gita, nineteen verses the gopis sing in his absence. Braj keeps the night as Sharad Purnima.

### Are the Radha Krishna stories real?

It depends what you are asking. The places are real and you can walk them, from Mathura and Vrindavan to Barsana and Govardhan hill. The texts are devotional literature rather than dated chronicle, and scholars date the texts, not the events. Devotees hold the lilas to be eternal and not fixed to one point in history. We tell you which text says what, so you can weigh it yourself.

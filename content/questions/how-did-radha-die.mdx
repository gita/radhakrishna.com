Short answer first. No classical Hindu scripture tells us how Radha died. The oldest major Krishna text, the Bhagavata Purana, does not even mention her by name. The Puranas that do give her a full role, the Brahma Vaivarta Purana and the Garga Samhita, describe [Radha and Krishna](/radha-krishna) as eternally united in Goloka, so she is a goddess rather than a woman who ages and dies. The tender scene most people picture, Radha slipping away as Krishna plays his flute, is a later retelling, not a verse.

I went looking for the verse, expecting to find it. There isn't one. That absence is the honest answer, and it is more interesting than the story that gets copied around.

## Does any scripture say how Radha died?

No. Not one classical text narrates Radha's death.

The reason surprises people. In the Bhagavata Purana, the source most Krishna stories rest on, Radha is not named at all. Scholars have long noted this. She surfaces as a distinct, central figure only later, above all in Jayadeva's Gita Govinda in the 12th century, which is the poem that fixed Radha in the popular imagination.

So the texts that could have described her death mostly do not describe her as a mortal in the first place. The later texts that exalt her, the Brahma Vaivarta Purana and the Garga Samhita, place her beyond death by design. She is Krishna's eternal half, dwelling with him in Goloka.

## What do the scriptures actually say, text by text?

Here is what each named source says when you read it for Radha's end, rather than for the story you expect.

| Text or tradition | What it says about Radha's death | How to read it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bhagavata Purana (c. 9th to 10th c.) | Does not name Radha. Silent on any death. Records one reunion of the Vrindavan gopis with Krishna at Kurukshetra during a solar eclipse (10.82). | Explicitly in scripture, by its silence |
| Brahma Vaivarta Purana | Radha is Krishna's eternal consort and mula-prakriti, dwelling with him forever in Goloka. Divine, not a mortal who dies. | Explicitly in a named scripture |
| Garga Samhita | Radha and Krishna are eternally united in Goloka. It narrates their union, not a death. | Explicitly in a named scripture |
| Gita Govinda (Jayadeva, 12th c.) | The work that made Radha central. A poem of love and longing. It does not narrate her death. | Later devotional literature |
| Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition | Radha is nitya, eternal, the hladini shakti, the bliss potency of Krishna. Her death does not apply. | Taught within a specific tradition |
| Popular and folk retelling | Aged Radha reaches Dwarka, asks Krishna to play the flute, and leaves her body or merges into him as he plays. | Later folk and devotional retelling, not in a classical text |

Notice the pattern. The named scriptures either stay silent or place Radha beyond death. Only the unsourced retelling gives you a death scene, and it names no chapter and verse.

## Where does the flute-at-Dwarka story come from?

You have probably read it. In old age Radha travels to Dwarka. Krishna, now a king, sees her. She asks him for one thing, to hear his flute one last time. He plays, and as the sound fills the air she closes her eyes and merges into him.

It is a beautiful story. It is also a devotional retelling, carried by blogs, videos, and lifestyle articles, not a passage from a Purana. I could not find it as a verse in any classical text, and the sources that repeat it do not cite one either.

That does not make it worthless. It carries a real theological idea from the Vaishnava traditions, that Radha's whole being is oriented to Krishna, and that her end is union rather than loss. The honest way to tell it is to name it for what it is, later devotion, not ancient scripture.

## Did Radha die before Krishna, or after he left Vrindavan?

Scripture does not answer this, because it never stages her death to begin with.

Krishna's own departure from the world is described, in the Bhagavata Purana's eleventh book. Radha has no matching scene. The Bhagavata simply goes quiet about the people of Vrindavan after Krishna leaves for Mathura. Into that silence, later storytellers wrote a Radha who stays in Braj, carries Krishna in her heart for decades, and is finally reunited with him. Whether she died first, or last, or at all, is a question the texts do not take up.

## Did Radha and Krishna ever meet again?

Once, in the reading most scholars point to. The Bhagavata Purana 10.82 describes a total solar eclipse that draws crowds to Kurukshetra. Krishna comes from Dwarka. The cowherds and women of Vrindavan come from Braj, and they meet Krishna again after many years. Radha is not named, but this is the closest thing scripture offers to a reunion after the Vrindavan years, and it is quiet and grave rather than romantic.

In the Brahma Vaivarta Purana and the Garga Samhita there is nothing to reunite, because Radha and Krishna were never truly parted. They are one in Goloka. This is why, in the Gaudiya tradition and in the [Hare Krishna maha-mantra](/mantras/hare-krishna-maha-mantra), Radha is invoked as Krishna's eternal energy, not remembered as someone who died.

## Frequently asked questions

**How did Radha die, and at what age?**

No scripture records Radha's death or gives her an age. The Bhagavata Purana does not name her, and the Puranas that do, the Brahma Vaivarta and Garga Samhita, treat her as an eternal goddess in Goloka. Any specific age or manner of death you see quoted online comes from folk retellings, not from a named text.

**Where did Radha die?**

No classical text names a place of Radha's death, because no classical text narrates it. The popular version sets her final moment at Dwarka, in Krishna's presence, as he plays the flute. That setting belongs to later devotional storytelling and is not stated in any Purana verse.

**Did Radha die before Krishna?**

Scripture does not say, because it never describes Radha dying at all. Krishna's own departure from the world is described in the Bhagavata Purana (Book 11), but Radha has no death scene to place before or after it. The idea that she died first is a feature of folk retellings, not of the texts.

**Did Radha die after Krishna left Vrindavan?**

The Bhagavata Purana is silent on what happened to Radha after Krishna left Vrindavan for Mathura. It records one reunion, when the women of Vrindavan meet Krishna at Kurukshetra during a solar eclipse (10.82), but it names no death. Later stories fill the gap with a life of longing and a final merging into Krishna.

**What happened to Radha after Krishna married Rukmini?**

The classical texts do not track Radha into Krishna's Dwarka years. Later devotional literature says she remained in Braj, held Krishna in her heart through decades of separation, and was reunited with him in Goloka rather than parted by his marriages. This is theology and folklore, not a scriptural event.

**Did Radha and Krishna reunite before she died?**

The one reunion scripture records is in the Bhagavata Purana 10.82, where the gopis of Vrindavan meet Krishna again at Kurukshetra during a solar eclipse, decades after he left. Radha is not named there. In the Brahma Vaivarta Purana and Garga Samhita there is no separation to heal, because Radha and Krishna are eternally one in Goloka.

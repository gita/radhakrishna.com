Krishna did not marry Radha in the Bhagavata Purana, the most authoritative account of his life, which never even names her. In that text, Krishna leaves Vrindavan for Mathura and later marries Rukmini and his queens in Dwarka. Other texts and traditions answer differently, and the honest answer is that it depends on which one you read.

There is no single clean reason, and that is the honest answer. The texts disagree with each other, and the disagreement is the real story. What follows sets each source beside the others, so you can see which tradition is speaking and what kind of claim it is making.

## Did Radha and Krishna ever marry?

In the canonical scripture, no. The Bhagavata Purana is the primary source for Krishna's life, and Radha does not appear in it by name. The famous Rasa Lila, Krishna's night of dancing with the gopis, is told across Canto 10, chapters 29 to 33, and the most beloved gopi in it is never called Radha.

Krishna then leaves Vrindavan for Mathura and never returns. His marriages happen afterward, in Dwarka, to Rukmini and the other queens. The Vrindavan love and the Dwarka marriages are two different chapters of his life, and Radha lives entirely in the first one.

Later devotional texts tell it differently, and some of them do describe a wedding. That is where the nuance lives, so the table below lays it out text by text.

## What does each text and tradition say about a Radha Krishna marriage?

Radha's story is not fixed across Hindu scripture. It grew over roughly a thousand years, from an unnamed gopi into the central figure of Krishna devotion. Different texts and sampradayas therefore give different answers, and being clear about which is which is the whole point.

So "they never married" is true of Krishna's earthly life as the Bhagavata Purana tells it. It is not the whole picture once you include the [Radha Krishna](/radha-krishna) devotional tradition that came later.

## Why did Krishna marry Rukmini and not Radha?

Because they belong to different stages of his life. Radha belongs to Vrindavan, to Krishna's youth among the cowherds. Rukmini belongs to Dwarka, to Krishna the king. He marries her after leaving Vrindavan behind, in the account at Bhagavata Purana 10.52 to 10.54, where she sends him a letter and he carries her away before an arranged wedding to Shishupala.

Rukmini becomes his chief queen. Tradition counts eight principal queens, the Ashtabharya, and 16,100 more women he rescued and married, for the well-known figure of 16,108 wives. Radha is not among them, and in the Bhagavata Purana she is not there to be counted.

The Gaudiya reading turns the question around. Krishna's married love in Dwarka is svakiya, love within social sanction. His love with the gopis in Vrindavan is parakiya, love that stands outside it. Chaitanya Charitamrita (Madhya 13.24) treats the parakiya love of Vrindavan as the higher of the two, precisely because it is given freely and asks for nothing in return.

## Was Radha married to someone else?

In later tradition, yes, and the three names people give are not three names for one man. They come from three different literatures.

The Brahma Vaivarta Purana calls him **Rayana**, a cowherd and the brother of Yashoda, and then takes the marriage back in the same chapter: only a shadow Radha, a chhaya, went through that wedding, while Radha herself disappeared. Later in the text Radha says it in her own voice, that she is only by reflection the wife of Rayana. **Abhimanyu** comes from somewhere else entirely, a sixteenth century Gaudiya handbook by Rupa Goswami, not from any Purana. **Ayan** is the Bengali form, from vernacular poetry. The Bhagavata Purana and the Gita Govinda, the two texts cited most often for this, give Radha no husband at all.

This is what makes the Gaudiya framing possible. If Radha is bound to another household, then her love for Krishna is parakiya by definition, love that risks everything and expects nothing. In this reading, a wedding would have made the love smaller, not larger. You can follow more of this in their [love story](/stories) and in the other [questions](/questions) devotees ask about them.

## Is there a deeper reason Krishna and Radha did not marry?

The theological answer is that a marriage would not describe what they are. In Gaudiya Vaishnava thought, Radha is not one more woman Krishna might have wed. She is his hladini shakti, his own pleasure potency, his self turned outward so that love can happen at all. Two who are already one being do not need a ceremony to join them.

Not every tradition frames it that way. The Nimbarka sampradaya holds that Radha and Krishna are eternally wed in the divine realm, so for them the marriage is real, it simply is not an earthly event. Even within the Gaudiya line, Jiva Gosvami accepted a svakiya (married) reading for some devotees, as noted in Chaitanya Charitamrita (Adi 10.85). The point that survives every version is the same: their union is treated as eternal, whatever name is put on it.

That is the honest shape of it. The question assumes a wedding was expected and withheld. The scriptures, read together, suggest the love was never the kind that a wedding could contain.

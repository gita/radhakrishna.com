faq:
  - question: "Is Radha mentioned in any Hindu scripture?"
    answer: >
      Yes, but not where most people look first. The Bhagavata Purana, the main
      scriptural life of Krishna, never names her. She is named in Jayadeva's
      Gita Govinda in the 12th century, and given a full theology in the Brahma
      Vaivarta Purana and the Garga Samhita. One of the oldest surviving
      mentions of her name sits in the Prakrit anthology Gatha Saptashati.

      Commentators also read her into Bhagavata Purana 10.30.28, the verse about
      the one gopi who had worshipped Krishna perfectly, though the text itself
      does not name her.
  - question: "Are Radha and Rukmini the same person?"
    answer: >
      No. Rukmini is the princess of Vidarbha who becomes Krishna's chief queen
      in Dwarka, and her letter, her elopement and her marriage are told at
      length in the Bhagavata Purana, Canto 10, chapters 52 to 54. Radha belongs
      to Vrindavan and to Krishna's youth, and she is not named in that Purana
      at all.

      Popular writing sometimes merges them, and Gaudiya theology relates them
      by calling Radha Krishna's hladini shakti (Chaitanya Charitamrita,
      Adi-lila 4.95-96), but no named text says the two are one woman.
  - question: "Can you worship Krishna without Radha?"
    answer: >
      Yes, and whole traditions do. At Udupi in Karnataka the Madhva line
      worships him as Balakrishna alone. Guruvayur in Kerala worships him as
      Guruvayurappan. At Puri he is worshipped as Jagannath alongside his
      brother Balabhadra and sister Subhadra.

      Worshipping the two together is the mark of the Braj-centred schools,
      Nimbarka, Gaudiya, Radha Vallabh and Pushtimarg, where Radha is
      inseparable from Krishna. Neither approach cancels the other.
  - question: "Why do people say Radhe Radhe, and why does Radha's name come first?"
    answer: >
      Radhe is the vocative form of Radha, so Radhe Radhe literally means O
      Radha, O Radha. It is the everyday greeting of the Braj region, painted on
      house walls and tree trunks around Vrindavan and Barsana, and Jai Shri
      Radhe is the temple variant.

      Her name goes first for a theological reason. In these schools the way to
      Krishna runs through his devotee, so you reach the Lord by calling his
      beloved.
  - question: "Was Radha older than Krishna?"
    answer: >
      No named scripture says so, because no named scripture gives Radha an age
      at all. The one age the texts do give is Krishna's. The Bhagavata Purana
      10.45.3 says he lived eleven years in Nanda's cowherd settlement before
      leaving for Mathura, and Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura's commentary puts
      his departure at ten years and seven months.

      The age gaps quoted online, five years or eleven months, have no verse
      behind them, and the traditions that develop Radha most fully treat her as
      eternal rather than older or younger.

This is where we answer the questions people actually ask about Radha and Krishna, one page per question. Krishna is the cowherd God of Vrindavan and Radha is his eternal beloved and foremost devotee. Every answer here is traced to a named text, cited by chapter and verse, labelled by tradition where the traditions disagree, and honest about where scripture ends and later devotion begins.

Most of these questions look simple and are not. That is the whole reason this section exists.

## Key takeaways

- Almost every hard question here traces back to one fact: the Bhagavata Purana never names Radha, so most claims about her rest on later texts.
- The texts that do name her are Jayadeva's Gita Govinda (12th century), the Brahma Vaivarta Purana and the Garga Samhita, with an early mention in the Prakrit Gatha Saptashati.
- The same question gets different answers in different sampradayas. Gaudiya, Nimbarka, Radha Vallabh and Pushtimarg do not agree, and each page tells you which school is speaking.
- The five most asked: why Krishna did not marry Radha, how Radha died, why Krishna is blue, whether Radha is Lakshmi, and why he wears a peacock feather.
- Where a popular answer has no verse behind it, the page tells you, rather than repeating it.

## Why does the same question get different answers?

Because this material sits in layers, and different schools stand on different layers.

The oldest and most authoritative account of Krishna's life, the Bhagavata Purana, tells the whole story and never names Radha once. Commentators read her into a single verse, 10.30.28, where the gopis realise that one among them had worshipped Krishna perfectly, since he slipped away from the rest to be alone with her. The word there is aradhita, worshipped, and many readers hear her name inside it. The text still does not say it.

Her named portrait arrives later. Jayadeva's Gita Govinda, a Sanskrit poem of the 12th century, makes her the heroine and fixes her in the imagination of every century after. The Brahma Vaivarta Purana and the Garga Samhita then give her a birth, a family and a theology. Older still, one of the earliest surviving mentions of her name sits in the Prakrit anthology Gatha Saptashati.

So when someone asks whether Radha and Krishna were married, the honest first move is a question back. According to which text?

[Why Krishna did not marry Radha](/questions/why-did-krishna-not-marry-radha) is the cleanest example. The Bhagavata Purana records no marriage. The Garga Samhita describes a wedding in the Bhandiravan forest with Brahma officiating. The Nimbarka tradition holds them eternally wed. Gaudiya Vaishnavas teach that their love was deliberately kept outside marriage and is higher for it. All four are real positions held by real schools. Flattening them into one confident sentence is how the internet gets this wrong.

## What you can expect from an answer here

Every answer on this page names its scripture by chapter and verse, so you can open the text and read
it for yourself.

Where the schools differ, you will see whose reading you are getting. Gaudiya, Nimbarka, Vallabha,
Radha Vallabh and Madhva teachers read the same events differently, and that difference is part of the
answer rather than something to tidy away.

You will also see the difference between a Purana and a much loved retelling. Some of the most
beautiful Radha Krishna stories come from later devotional literature or from the oral tradition of
Braj. That takes nothing away from them. It simply tells you what kind of source you are holding.

And where the texts are silent, the page says so. Radha's passing is the clearest example. The
scriptures do not narrate it, however confidently the internet does.

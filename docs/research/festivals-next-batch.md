## Govardhan Puja / Annakut

**Dates.** Kartik shukla pratipada, the day after Diwali.

| Year | Date                    | Both authorities                            |
| ---- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| 2026 | **Tuesday 10 November** | Drik Panchang and the ISKCON calendar agree |
| 2027 | **Saturday 30 October** | Drik Panchang and the ISKCON calendar agree |

Pratahkala muhurat 2026: 06:40 to 08:50. Pratipada begins 12:31 on 9 Nov, ends 14:00 on 10 Nov.

**Watch the location trap.** Drik Panchang recomputes per city and returns 9 November for Mountain
View, California. **Pin to Delhi.** Sites publishing 9 November for India have not pinned.

**Why the date is not simply "the day after Diwali".** Pratipada spans two sunrises in 2026, so the
classical rule decides: if the moon would rise during the puja window at sunset, the puja moves to the
day before. Worth explaining on the page, since "What is the real date of Govardhan Puja?" is a live
PAA question.

**The lila.** Bhagavata Purana 10.24 to 10.27.

- **10.24.25**, the pivot: "Therefore may a sacrifice for the pleasure of the cows, the brāhmaṇas and
  Govardhana Hill begin! With all the paraphernalia collected for worshiping Indra, let this sacrifice
  be performed instead."
- **10.24.26** is the seed of Annakut: cook every kind of preparation, "beginning with sweet rice,
  porridge, buns and cakes", using all the dairy.
- **10.24.32-33**: they offered with Vedic mantras, **gave grass to the cows**, then
  **circumambulated the hill** with the cattle walking in front. This is the root of the parikrama.
- **10.24.35**: Krishna assumes a huge form and declares **"I am Govardhana Mountain!"** and eats the
  offerings.
- **10.25.19**, the lifting: "Having said this, Lord Kṛṣṇa, who is Viṣṇu Himself, picked up Govardhana
  Hill with one hand and held it aloft just as easily as a child holds up a mushroom."
- **10.25.23**: he held it seven days without eating or drinking.
- **10.26.3**: he was seven years old.
- **10.27.21-23**: Surabhi bathes him in her milk, Indra bathes him with Ganga water from Airavata's
  trunk, and **gives him the name Govinda**. The story ends in reconciliation, not defeat.

**Say "with one hand", not "on his little finger".** The verse reads _ekena hastena_, and the
Hari-vamsa line in the purport says the left hand. The little finger is Braj devotional idiom and
Pushtimarg iconography rather than the Bhagavatam text.

**Annakut.** _Anna-kuta_, a mountain of food, offered as the hill itself. Chhappan bhog, 56 items,
from the tradition that Krishna took eight meals a day and went seven days without: 8 x 7.

**Shrinathji at Nathdwara** has the most famous Annakut: a mound of rice representing the hill in the
Doltibari, a great sweet gunja on top, cows decorated with peacock plumes and ghungharu on their
hooves, and only two public darshans that day because the day is so full. The svarupa itself is the
raised **left arm** of Govardhannathji, seen emerging from the hill in 1466, worshipped because
Krishna lifted the hill with his left hand.

**Govardhan hill.** An 8 km sandstone ridge in Mathura district, about 30 m at its highest. The
parikrama is **21 km**, five to seven hours on foot, usually begun at Mansi Ganga, often barefoot.
Badi (outer) 12 km, chhoti (inner) 9 km. Stops: Mansi Ganga, Danghati, Govind Kund, Radha Kund and
Shyam Kund, Kusum Sarovar, Jatipura (Mukharavind), Punchari ka Lautha.

**The hill as a peacock at rest**, a lovely frame for the page: face at Kusum Sarovar, neck at Manasi
Ganga, mouth at Mukharavind, the two eyes Radha Kund and Shyam Kund, tail ending at Punchari.

**Why devotees do not climb it.** Vallabhacharya did not walk on the hill during his 1515 parikrama,
holding Govardhan to be non-different from Krishna, and Vaishnavas have not stepped on it since. The
doctrinal ground is 10.24.35. Some perform dandavati parikrama, full obeisances at every step, which
takes days.

**At home.** A Govardhan shaped from cow dung on the floor, decorated with flowers and grass, with
small cow figures around it; lamps lit; the family circumambulates it; Annakut cooked and offered;
cows washed, tilaked, garlanded and fed.

## Gopashtami

**Dates.** Kartik shukla ashtami, seven days after Govardhan Puja.

| Year | Date                    | Both authorities                            |
| ---- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| 2026 | **Tuesday 17 November** | Drik Panchang and the ISKCON calendar agree |
| 2027 | **Saturday 6 November** | Drik Panchang and the ISKCON calendar agree |

No fixed puja muhurat is published; it is a daytime observance.

**What it is.** The day Krishna was entrusted with the **cows**, having until then tended only the
**calves**. His promotion from vatsapa to gopa, and the first day he led the herd out.

**The citation is unusually clean.** The lila is at **Bhagavata Purana 10.15.1**, where Rama and
Krishna reach pauganda age and the cowherd men let them take up tending the cows. The **naming of the
day** is in the Padma Purana's Kartika-mahatmya, quoted in the purport:

> "The eighth lunar day of the bright fortnight of the month of Kārttika is known by authorities as
> Gopāṣṭamī. From that day, Lord Vāsudeva served as a cowherd, whereas previously He had tended the
> calves."

So the Bhagavatam gives the event and the Padma Purana gives the day. State it that way; it is a
strong, positive thing to be able to say.

Lovely detail from the purport: _padaih_, he blessed the earth by walking it barefoot, which gave the
girls of Vrindavan anxiety over his soft feet in the thorny forest.

**Radharani's lotus feet.** On Gopashtami many temples in Vrindavan show Radharani with her **lotus
feet visible**, the one day of the year, because she is dressed as a **gwala**, a cowherd boy, in
dhoti, kurta and pagdi with a rope in her hand, to go out with the herd. Her feet are covered every
other day.

**Do not place this at Barsana.** Every news-grade source ties the Barsana Shri Ji charan darshan to
**Radha Ashtami**, not Gopashtami. Write the Vrindavan and Gaudiya temple practice, which is solid.

**Cow worship, the actual sequence.** Bathe and clean the cows before sunrise; tilak of roli, kumkum
and haldi on the forehead; garland and dress them; aarti with a ghee diya in the goshala; feed green
fodder, jaggery, fruit, gram and panchamrit; wash the cow's feet and do parikrama around her; walk
with the herd a little way; take the dust of the hooves as tilak; go-seva and go-dana. Bhog commonly
aloo puri, sooji halwa, kheer.

**Nandgaon is the strongest location**, kept as the site of Krishna's first cow-grazing, with a
gau-charan lila and procession. Also Vrindavan, Mathura, Barsana, Govardhan and Nathdwara.

## UNVERIFIED, do not publish

- The Barsana Ladli ji charan darshan on **Gopashtami**. Social media only; news sources place it on
  Radha Ashtami.
- The **Radha and Subala clothes-exchange** story as the reason for the gwala vesh. Attributed by one
  outlet to the Bhagavatam; **it is not in the Bhagavatam**. Gaudiya lila literature. Needs a textual
  source before use.
- A temple-by-temple list of which Vrindavan temples show the feet. Radha Damodar and ISKCON appear in
  devotee accounts; Banke Bihari, Radha Raman and Radha Vallabh are not confirmed.
- The **Pulastya Muni** figures for the hill's original size and its rate of shrinking. Sources
  disagree. Use the account as tradition, publish no numbers.
- **Banke Bihari Annakut** specifics. Nothing news-grade found.
- The 2026 **Sayankala muhurat** for Delhi. Only the Pratahkala window was returned on fetch. Both
  windows are confirmed for 2027.
- The **84 kos** question in PAA conflates Govardhan parikrama with the Braj Chaurasi Kos Yatra.

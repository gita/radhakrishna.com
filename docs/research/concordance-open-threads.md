# Concordance: open threads

Claims that could not be corroborated to two independent sources, and are therefore **not** in the
dataset. Kept here so they are chased rather than quietly forgotten, and so nobody re-researches the
same dead ends. Nothing on this page is published anywhere on the site.

Version of the concordance when written: 1.0 (2026-07-25).

## Resolved

**Brahma Vaivarta, Prakriti Khanda 49: Rayana and the shadow Radha. CONFIRMED, verified directly.**
Two researchers disagreed about this. One corroborated it, the other could reach only weak sources and
dropped it. It is load-bearing for the husband page, so the full English translation on archive.org was
downloaded and read at the passage rather than taking either side. Chapter 49 carries all of it:

- "out of the twelve cowherds, there was a cowherd named Rayana-gopa who happened to be a ray of lord
  Krsna"
- "she was married to a trader named Rayana; at the time of marriage only a shadow Radha was married to
  the trader while the real Radha herself disappeared"
- "This Rayana was the real brother of Yasodha the mother of Krsna ... Brahma the creator of the universe
  had performed the marriage of Radha with Krsna"
- "her shadow was very much present in the house of Rayana"
- Radha's own voice, to Nanda in the Bhandira grove: "I am Radha myself and by reflection the wife of
  Rayana"
- the curse: "I have become the daughter of Vrsabhana because of the curse of Sridama"

Source: the Nagar / Parimal English translation, all four khandas, at
`archive.org/details/brahma-vaivarta-purana-all-four-kandas-english-translation`, Prakriti Khanda ch. 49,
pp. 415 to 418. Note the translation spells the cursing cowherd both Sudama and Sridama in the same
chapter. Verse numbers are still not established, so the rows cite khanda and chapter only.

## Worth a second pass, could change a page

**Mahabhagavata Purana 49.16-22 and 51.33-34.** The _Indian Historical Quarterly_ vol. 24 (1948) quotes
Sanskrit and cites these for Radha being married to Ayana Gopa, an emanation of Shiva who then loses his
virility. This is the only Sanskrit Purana-class text found that uses the name **Ayana**, which would
change the husband page: at present Ayan is attributed entirely to Bengali vernacular literature. Single
sourced, and the text itself is not readable online. It is a Shakta upapurana of Bengal, dated 10th to
11th century by Wikipedia and 12th to 13th by Hazra. **Needed:** a readable edition or translation.

**Devi Bhagavata Purana, book 9.** Its ninth skandha parallels the Brahma Vaivarta Prakriti Khanda
closely, so it may carry its own version of the Rayana episode. Chapters 49 and 50 were checked and do
not contain it. The rest of book 9 has not been swept.

**Verse numbers inside Brahma Vaivarta Prakriti Khanda ch. 49.** The chapter is solid and corroborated
(Shanti Lal Nagar / Parimal edition, plus an independent academic paper citing the same content). What is
missing is a numbered shloka, and chapter numbering shifts between the Nagar, Rajendra Nath Sen and
Bengali recensions. **Do not add a verse number without a Sanskrit edition in hand.** The rows cite
khanda and chapter only, on purpose.

## Settled as negative findings

**The lexicon gloss "wife of Ayanaghosha".** Monier-Williams was checked directly and does **not** carry
it: s.v. _rādhā_ reads only "a celebrated cowherdess or Gopī (beloved by Kṛṣṇa)". A doctoral study of the
Gita Govinda asserts that _a_ Sanskrit lexicon defines Radha this way, but which one (Shabdakalpadruma,
Vachaspatyam, Apte, Trikandashesha) is not established. No row names a dictionary, and the Gita Govinda
row says the claim comes from later dictionary entries rather than from the poem.

**"Rapan".** Every edition consulted reads Rayana (रायाण). No edition reading Rapan was found, but no
source explicitly documents it as a misreading either, so the row says "editions read Rayana" rather than
asserting the misreading.

## Loose ends of lower consequence

- **Ganoddesa-dipika text 174.** Two independent hosts carry identical wording, so the content is
  corroborated. Not pinned: whether the numbering is continuous or per-vibhaga, and whether "so-called
  husband" renders a qualifier in Rupa Goswami's Sanskrit or is the translator's gloss. The Sanskrit was
  not located.
- **Nimbarka Dashashloki verse number.** Numbered 5 by two sources and 6 by two others. The row omits the
  number. The attribution of the Dashashloki to Nimbarkacharya himself is also disputed by some scholars.
- **Garga Samhita dating.** "Late medieval, commonly placed in the 16th to 17th century CE" is a working
  range. Check against Rocher, _The Purāṇas_.
- **Skanda Purana Reva Khanda dating.** Generally treated as later than the 12th century; no scholarly
  range specific to this khanda was found. The verse itself is solid at 5.3.198.76.
- **Whether the Matsya Purana also carries the "rukmini dvaravatyam" line.** One aggregator says so; only
  the Skanda location is verifiable.
- **Vallabhacharya's Subodhini and Radha.** The claim that Vallabha never names Radha in his Bhagavata
  commentary rests on a single Pushtimarg site. The Pushtimarg row states only what is confirmed.
- **Gaudiya raganuga practice setting aside dvaraka-dhyana.** Found on gosai.com, not traceable to a
  numbered verse in Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu or Hari-bhakti-vilasa. Left out.

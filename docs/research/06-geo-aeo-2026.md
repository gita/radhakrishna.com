# GEO / AEO in 2026: How to Get Cited by ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews/AI Mode, Gemini & Claude

**Research date:** 24 July 2026
**Scope:** Latest developments (roughly April–July 2026), with the durable fundamentals that still hold.
**Audience:** A devotional / informational content site (radhakrishna.net).

---

## Executive summary (TL;DR)

1. **AI citation is earned by structure + evidence, not by markup.** Google's own May 2026 guide says there is no special schema, no AI text file, and no AI-specific content trick required — the same "helpful content" foundations drive AI Overviews and AI Mode.
2. **The three highest-leverage content moves (Princeton GEO study, still the reference):** add direct **quotations** (+27.8% visibility), add **statistics/data** (+25.9%), and **cite named sources** (+24.9%). These beat keyword stuffing, which _hurt_.
3. **Answer-first structure wins.** Lead each `H2`/`H3` (phrased as the user's question) with a self-contained 40–75 word answer, add a TL;DR/Key-Takeaways block in the first 150–200 words, and keep one idea per paragraph.
4. **Tables and lists get extracted disproportionately** — pages with comparison tables were cited ~4.2× more than the same data in prose. Definition blocks and consistent entity naming matter.
5. **Schema is a supporting act, not the driver.** An Ahrefs study of 1,885 pages found **zero measurable citation uplift** from adding JSON-LD. Google **removed FAQ rich results on 7 May 2026** and deprecated HowTo years ago. Still worth keeping: **Organization, Article, Person(author), BreadcrumbList, ImageObject** wired together with `@id`/`sameAs`.
6. **llms.txt is effectively dead for discovery.** Mueller compared it to the keywords meta tag; 97% of llms.txt files got zero traffic. Skip it (only useful for API/developer docs).
7. **Author authority is now a first-class signal.** Google added an "Authors" section to Search Central (Feb 2026); pages with named, entity-resolved authors are cited materially more often. Build real author pages + `Person` schema with `sameAs`.
8. **Google's 2026 core/spam updates punish _scaled content without proportional value_, not AI use per se.** Sites publishing volumes of unedited AI pages lost 50–80% of traffic. AI-assisted content that is fact-checked, sourced, and edited is safe.
9. **Off-site presence drives AI citations more than most on-site tweaks.** Reddit, YouTube, Wikipedia/Wikidata and topical forums are top-cited domains; being _mentioned and corroborated_ across the web (entity consistency) is what LLMs trust.
10. **Architecture: hub-and-spoke topic clusters** (pillar + spokes, tight internal linking, semantic HTML) produce ~3.2× more AI citations than standalone posts — the ideal shape for a devotional site organized around deities, scriptures, festivals, and practices.

## 1. What is actually driving AI citations right now (2026), and what changed

### The durable finding

The Princeton/Georgia Tech **"GEO" study** remains the most-cited empirical baseline, and 2026 practitioner data (HubSpot State of AEO 2026; Writesonic; AirOps) keeps confirming it. The methods that measurably raise how often LLMs cite you:

| Tactic                                           | Measured visibility lift |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------ |
| Add direct **quotations** (from experts/sources) | **+27.8%**               |
| Add **statistics / numeric data**                | **+25.9%**               |
| **Cite sources** (name them, link them)          | **+24.9%**               |
| Add authoritative language / fluency             | modest positive          |
| **Keyword stuffing**                             | **negative** (hurts)     |

### What changed in 2026 (last few months)

- **Google published its first official generative-AI optimization guide (15 May 2026)** and said plainly: no special schema, no AI text files, no AI-only rewrites, no micro-chunking are required — normal helpful, crawlable content is what feeds AI Overviews and AI Mode. ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/05/a-new-resource-for-optimizing))
- **Preferred Sources rolled out to AI Overviews & AI Mode (late May 2026):** users can nominate sources they want to see more of, plus Google added a **"frequently cited" label**, a **perspectives carousel**, and social/forum sources for opinion queries. Being a repeat, trusted citation now compounds. ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-ai-mode-gain-preferred-sources-plus-new-perspectives-carousel-and-highly-cited-labels-478766))
- **Google rank is still the upstream filter for AI Mode** — top-ranked pages are far more likely to be pulled, even though URL overlap between organic top-10 and AI citations is loose. Classic SEO fundamentals remain the ticket to the draw.
- **Off-site citation share shifted:** after Reddit sued Perplexity (Oct 2025), Perplexity's Reddit citations dropped ~86% and **YouTube** filled much of the gap. YouTube is now the single most-cited domain in AI search overall; Reddit still ranks #1 across the blended engines in Peec AI's 30M-source analysis. ([Contently](https://contently.com/2026/04/29/top-sources-llms-cite/), [Everything-PR](https://everything-pr.com/perplexity-citation-source-index-2026))
- **Low cross-engine overlap:** an audit of 680M citations found only ~11% of domains are cited by _both_ ChatGPT and Perplexity — you must optimize for a spread of engines, not one. ([AuthorityTech](https://authoritytech.io/curated/ai-citation-11-percent-platform-overlap-per-engine-audit-2026))

## 2. Schema.org / structured data for AI citation

### The blunt truth (2026)

- Structured data is **not required** for AI Overviews / AI Mode (Google, May 2026).
- **Ahrefs** tracked 1,885 pages that added JSON-LD (Aug 2025–Mar 2026) and measured **zero meaningful citation uplift** on Google AI Overviews, AI Mode, or ChatGPT. ([AuthorityTech summary](https://authoritytech.io/curated/schema-markup-ai-citations-ahrefs-study-2026))
- So schema is **hygiene and entity-plumbing**, not a citation lever. Keep it because it powers rich results, Knowledge Graph/entity resolution, and helps _non-Google_ assistants parse content — but don't expect it to move citations by itself.

### What to use — and how (current best practice)

| Type                         | Verdict 2026                                         | Use it for                                                                                                                     |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Organization**             | Keep (foundational)                                  | Brand entity identity; `sameAs` to Wikipedia/Wikidata/social; logo, `@id` anchor for the whole graph                           |
| **Article / BlogPosting**    | Keep                                                 | `author`, `datePublished`, **`dateModified`** (recency signal for AI Overviews), `publisher`, `image`                          |
| **Person** (author)          | **Keep — now high value**                            | Author identity + `sameAs` (Wikipedia, Wikidata, LinkedIn, ORCID/registries); resolves the byline to a real entity             |
| **BreadcrumbList**           | Keep                                                 | Communicates site hierarchy / topical context to crawlers and AI                                                               |
| **ImageObject**              | Keep                                                 | Machine-readable image metadata (caption, creator, license, date) — feeds Google Images + multimodal AI                        |
| **QAPage**                   | Situational                                          | Only for genuinely user-generated Q&A threads (one question, many answers)                                                     |
| **FAQPage**                  | **Degraded** — rich result **removed 7 May 2026**    | Markup itself not deprecated; keep only if you have real Q&A. No FAQ rich snippet anymore; some non-Google LLMs still parse it |
| **HowTo**                    | **Deprecated** (desktop rich result gone since 2023) | Don't rely on it; write the steps as visible ordered content instead                                                           |
| **VideoObject**              | Keep / growing                                       | Given YouTube's citation dominance, mark up embedded/hosted video                                                              |
| **Review / AggregateRating** | Keep (where genuine)                                 | Quantified sentiment AI uses to compare — must be real, on-page                                                                |

### Wiring best practice

- Build **one connected graph** using `@graph`, `@id`, and `sameAs` so Organization ↔ Article ↔ Person ↔ WebPage reference each other. Disconnected snippets are weak; a linked entity graph is what helps LLMs disambiguate "who says this."
- **Schema must match visible content.** AI systems (and Google's spam systems) check markup-to-content consistency; mismatches are ignored or flagged.

## 3. Content structure that gets cited

The 2026 consensus "extraction-ready" patterns (Writesonic, AirOps, Kime, Pepper):

1. **Question-led headings.** Phrase `H2`/`H3` as the exact question a user would ask ("Who is Radha?", "What does the Hare Krishna mantra mean?"). This mirrors query syntax and marks the block as an answer candidate.
2. **Answer-first paragraph.** Immediately under each heading, give a **self-contained 40–75 word** direct answer that resolves the question without needing surrounding context. This is the single biggest extraction lever.
3. **TL;DR / Key Takeaways block** in the first screen (150–200 words), giving the LLM the whole page's gist up front.
4. **One idea per paragraph**, short (2–4 sentences), fact-dense sentences. Long diluted blocks get skipped.
5. **Standalone sections** — each section should make sense if lifted out on its own (LLMs chunk pages).
6. **Comparison tables** for any "X vs Y" or multi-attribute data — cited ~4.2× more than prose equivalents.
7. **Definition blocks** — define every key term ("What is _bhakti_?") in a clean term→definition pattern; do not assume jargon knowledge.
8. **Numbered/ordered lists** for processes, steps, rituals, sequences.
9. **Explicit, consistent entity naming.** Use the full canonical name repeatedly ("Radha Krishna", "Bhagavad Gita") rather than pronouns; keep terminology consistent across the page and site.
10. **Direct-answer + evidence pattern:** claim → statistic/quote/scripture citation → source. This stacks the Princeton levers (quote + stat + source) into one block.

Pages built this way are reported cited 2–4× more across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini and AI Overviews.

**Sources:** [Writesonic — Structure content for LLMs](https://writesonic.com/blog/how-to-structure-content-for-llms-citation-and-retrieval) · [Writesonic — 31-point citation-readiness checklist](https://medium.com/writesonic/the-31-point-citation-readiness-checklist-what-makes-ai-actually-cite-your-content-48642412eb4f) · [AirOps — question-based headings](https://www.airops.com/blog/question-based-headings-ai-citations) · [Kime — structure for LLM extraction](https://kime.ai/blog/structure-content-for-llm-extraction) · [Pepper — structure for AI citation](https://www.pepper.inc/blog/how-to-structure-content-for-ai-citation/)

## 4. llms.txt / ai.txt — current status

**Verdict: skip it for a content/devotional site.**

- **Google's John Mueller** likened llms.txt to the old keywords meta tag; said no AI services were using it and bots weren't even requesting the file; called building separate Markdown-for-bots pages a poor use of time. Google's May 2026 AI guide explicitly lists AI text files among things you can **ignore**. ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-says-llms-txt-is-purely-speculative-for-now/577576/), [Baseline Labs](https://baselinelabs.ai/blog/llms-txt-google-search))
- **Ahrefs:** across 137,000 sites, **97% of llms.txt files received zero traffic** (May 2026). No major lab (OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, Meta) has committed to reading it in production.
- **`ai.txt`** (a proposed crawler-permission/monetization file) is similarly unadopted; it is not a citation lever.
- **Only legitimate use case:** extensive **developer/API documentation** consumed by AI coding assistants — not applicable here.
- **What actually controls AI crawlers:** standard `robots.txt` (including the newer AI-bot user-agents like `GPTBot`, `PerplexityBot`, `Google-Extended`, `ClaudeBot`) and Google's own crawler controls. Manage those instead.

**Sources:** [Passionfruit — should I create llms.txt (Google 2026)](https://www.getpassionfruit.com/blog/should-i-create-an-llms.txt-file-google-s-2026-guidance-explained) · [Derivatex — llms.txt guide](https://derivatex.agency/blog/llms-txt-guide/)

## 5. E-E-A-T, author/authority signals, and citing primary sources

### Why it matters more in 2026

E-E-A-T now behaves as **both a ranking filter and an AI-visibility filter**. LLMs preferentially cite content tied to **verifiable human credentials** and entities they can corroborate elsewhere.

- **Google formalized authorship:** on **1 Feb 2026** it added an **"Authors" section** to Search Central documentation covering how it identifies authors and what constitutes authorship transparency. ([Contently](https://contently.com/2026/05/11/eeat-and-ai-search-author-credentials/))
- Practitioner data: pages **without named authors are reported ~40% less likely** to be cited by AI engines; schema-attributed authors whose entity records resolve outperform generic bylines in retrieval. ([SEO.com.ph](https://www.seo.com.ph/blog/author-authority-seo), [ClickRank](https://www.clickrank.ai/e-e-a-t-and-ai/))

### Concrete authority build (do this)

1. **Real author pages** for every contributor: photo, credentials, topical focus, links to their other work.
2. **`Person` schema with rich `sameAs`** → Wikipedia/Wikidata (if notable), LinkedIn, scholarly/registry profiles, so the author resolves to a real-world entity in the Knowledge Graph.
3. **Bylines on every article**, linked to the author page; add visible **"Reviewed by"** for sensitive spiritual/doctrinal content.
4. **Cite primary sources.** For a devotional site: quote **scripture with chapter/verse** (e.g., Bhagavad Gita 9.22), name translators/editions, link to authoritative texts and recognized institutions/temples/scholars. This directly triggers the Princeton "quote + cite source" levers and satisfies rater "reputation research."
5. **Entity consistency off-site:** the same brand/author names, described the same way, across Wikipedia, Wikidata, YouTube, reputable directories and mentions. AI trust comes from corroboration across independent sources, not from your site alone.
6. **Visible freshness:** show **"Last updated"** dates and keep `dateModified` accurate — HubSpot's State of AEO 2026 found visible update dates correlate with higher citation rates.

## 6. Google's 2026 core / helpful-content / spam updates: AI content, scaled abuse, thin content

### What Google did (2026 timeline)

- **March 2026 spam update:** directly targeted **scaled content abuse**, expired-domain abuse, and site-reputation abuse ("parasite SEO"). Sites publishing hundreds/thousands of unedited AI pages saw **50–80% traffic drops**.
- **March 2026 core update:** broad quality reassessment (also folded page-experience/Core Web Vitals more holistically).
- **May 2026 core update:** widely read as tightening quality thresholds in the AI-search era; layered on the new generative-AI policies.
- **June 2026 core update:** further hit scaled AI content lacking added value.

### The critical nuance

Google's scaled-content-abuse policy **does not prohibit AI-generated content.** The dividing line is **value, not authorship**:

- **Penalized:** volume _without proportional value_ — pages that exist to catch keywords, near-duplicate templated output, no editorial oversight, no original insight.
- **Safe:** AI-_assisted_ content that is **fact-checked, sourced, edited, enriched with original evidence, and written for a real audience** — even at scale, if each page genuinely serves intent.

### How to publish at volume safely (checklist)

- Every page answers a **real, distinct query/need** — no near-duplicates, no thin doorway pages.
- **Human editorial pass** on every published page: fact-check scripture/claims, add a primary-source citation, tighten.
- Add **original value per page**: a quote, an explanation, a comparison table, an image, first-hand interpretation — not just rephrased common knowledge.
- **Named author + reviewer**, visible dates.
- **Publish in themed clusters**, not scattered one-offs, so volume reads as topical depth rather than churn.
- **Prune/merge** thin or overlapping pages; consolidate rather than proliferate.
- Watch the ratio: don't 10× your page count faster than you can maintain quality — that pattern itself is a scaled-abuse signal.

## 7. Image & visual optimization for AI, Google Images & Pinterest

Google now pulls images into AI Overviews and featured snippets, and LLMs use image metadata to understand/cite visuals. The same signals serve Google Images and Pinterest.

**Do:**

- **Descriptive, factual alt text, ~80–125 characters**, primary keyword used once naturally (e.g., "Radha and Krishna under a kadamba tree, Kangra miniature painting"). Not keyword-stuffed.
- **Descriptive file names** (`radha-krishna-raas-lila.webp`, not `IMG_4821.jpg`).
- **`ImageObject` schema** with `caption`, `creator`/`author`, `contentUrl`, `license`/`acquireLicensePage`, `datePublished`, dimensions — machine-readable data alt text can't carry (esp. **licensing**, which Google surfaces).
- **Visible on-page captions** — AI and readers both use them for context; captioned images are extracted more.
- **Contextual placement** near the relevant text; images should illustrate the specific claim/section.
- **Next-gen formats (WebP/AVIF)** + compression for LCP/Core Web Vitals; responsive `srcset`.
- **Image sitemap** entries; ensure images are crawlable (not blocked, not lazy-loaded in a way bots miss).
- **Pinterest specifics:** vertical 2:3 (1000×1500) pins, keyword-rich pin titles + descriptions, Rich Pins (which pull your Open Graph/schema metadata), and a board structure that mirrors your topic clusters (deities, festivals, mantras, art).
- **Original imagery** where possible (original photos/art of temples, deities, festivals) — more citable and defensible than stock, and supports E-E-A-T "experience."

**Don't:** rely on embedded EXIF metadata for ranking (Google prioritizes alt text + structured data), leave decorative images with keyword-stuffed alt, or serve giant unoptimized files.

## 8. Site architecture for AI

### Hub-and-spoke / topic clusters / entity hubs

- **Pillar (hub) page** per major topic + **spoke pages** for sub-topics. Clustered content is reported to earn **~3.2× more AI citations** (and ~30% more organic traffic) than standalone posts.
- **Internal linking rule set:**
  - Every spoke links **up to its pillar**.
  - The pillar links **down to every spoke**.
  - Spokes link **laterally to 2–3 related spokes** in the same cluster.
  - Use **descriptive, entity-rich anchor text** (the topic name, not "click here").
- **Entity hubs:** treat major entities (each deity, each scripture, each festival) as a hub with a canonical page that defines the entity, then interlinks all related content. This mirrors how LLMs organize knowledge (entities + relationships) and builds the topical authority AI answer engines reward.
- **Semantic HTML:** proper `<article>`, `<section>`, `<nav>`, heading hierarchy (single `<h1>`, logical `<h2>/<h3>`), `<table>` for tabular data, `<ol>/<ul>` for steps/lists, `<figure>/<figcaption>` for images. Clean semantic structure makes chunking/extraction reliable — a repeatedly-cited 2026 structural factor.
- **Crawlability & rendering:** server-render or pre-render key content (don't hide it behind client-side JS that AI crawlers may not execute); fast pages; clean URL paths that reflect the cluster (`/deities/radha/`, `/scriptures/bhagavad-gita/chapter-9/`).

### Devotional-site cluster example

```
Hub: Radha Krishna (canonical entity page)
 ├─ Who is Radha? (definition + primary sources)
 ├─ Who is Krishna?
 ├─ The Raas Lila explained
 ├─ Hare Krishna mahamantra — meaning & practice
 ├─ Major festivals (Radhashtami, Janmashtami) → each its own spoke
 └─ Key scriptures (Bhagavad Gita, Bhagavata Purana) → linked entity hubs
```

## 9. Do's and Don'ts checklist — devotional / informational content site

### DO

- ✅ Lead every section with an **answer-first 40–75 word** direct response under a **question-style heading**.
- ✅ Add a **TL;DR / Key Takeaways** box near the top of every page.
- ✅ **Quote primary sources** — scripture with chapter/verse, named translators, recognized scholars/temples/institutions. (Biggest citation lever.)
- ✅ Include **statistics, dates, and specific facts** where relevant (festival dates, historical periods, verse counts).
- ✅ Use **comparison tables** and **definition blocks** for concepts, deities, festivals, philosophies.
- ✅ Put a **named author + credentials + `Person` schema (`sameAs`)** on every page; add a **"Reviewed by"** for doctrinal accuracy.
- ✅ Show **visible "Last updated" dates** and keep `dateModified` honest.
- ✅ Wire a **connected schema graph** (Organization ↔ Article ↔ Person ↔ Breadcrumb ↔ ImageObject) with `@id`/`sameAs`.
- ✅ Build **hub-and-spoke entity clusters** with disciplined internal linking and semantic HTML.
- ✅ Use **original imagery** with descriptive alt text, captions, and `ImageObject` (incl. license).
- ✅ Maintain **strong classic SEO** (rank, crawlability, speed) — it's the upstream filter for AI Mode.
- ✅ Build **off-site presence**: YouTube (now #1 cited), Wikipedia/Wikidata entity entries, reputable directories, genuine community/forum participation.
- ✅ **Edit and fact-check every page** before publishing, even AI-assisted drafts.
- ✅ Manage **`robots.txt`** for AI crawler user-agents intentionally (allow the ones you want citing you).

### DON'T

- ❌ Don't rely on **FAQPage/HowTo** for rich results — FAQ rich results were **removed 7 May 2026**; HowTo long deprecated.
- ❌ Don't waste time on **llms.txt / ai.txt** — no confirmed payoff for a content site.
- ❌ Don't expect **schema alone** to earn citations — it's plumbing, not a lever (Ahrefs: zero uplift).
- ❌ Don't **mass-publish unedited AI pages** — the fast lane to a scaled-content-abuse hit (50–80% drops).
- ❌ Don't publish **thin, near-duplicate, or doorway** pages; prune/merge instead.
- ❌ Don't **keyword-stuff** headings, body, or alt text — measurably _reduces_ AI visibility.
- ❌ Don't use **generic bylines** ("Admin", "Staff") or unnamed authors on doctrinal content.
- ❌ Don't let **markup contradict visible content** — it gets ignored/flagged.
- ❌ Don't bury key content behind **client-side JS** that crawlers may not render.
- ❌ Don't make **doctrinal claims without a citable source** — for a spiritual site this is both an accuracy risk (AI is increasingly trusted for spiritual advice) and a citation-quality risk.
